Professional Profile

Quantitative economist with peer-reviewed research in poverty measurement, microsimulation of policy interventions, and household-survey labour analysis, combined with a decade of high-level econometric practice across investment banking and macro fund management. Returning to public-policy research after the work I have always found most meaningful: rigorous, distributionally-grounded evidence aimed at reducing inequality and shifting power. Comfortable with complex household survey microdata, panel methods, and end-to-end quantitative pipelines — and equally comfortable communicating findings to non-technical audiences under tight deadlines.

Selected Achievements Mapped to the Role

  • Domain knowledge — social security & poverty: Three peer-reviewed papers on welfare measurement, microsimulation of policy reforms, and income/inequality dynamics, all built on national household survey microdata.
  • Domain knowledge — labour markets: Published macroeconomic analysis of labour-supply dynamics (US participation margins); applied labour-market diagnostics across Central & Eastern Europe at Deutsche Bank.
  • Quantitative methods (UK-relevant): Designed counterfactual policy simulations with SAS Maps/ADePT and STATA; built Bayesian and state-space models in PyMC, MATLAB (IRIS, Dynare), and EViews; familiarity with the UKMOD/EUROMOD microsimulation framework and Understanding Society (UKHLS) panel data.
  • Communication under pressure: Authored daily and weekly briefings to thousands of institutional subscribers at Deutsche Bank; delivered live-market commentary to portfolio managers at Soloda; led 700+ hours of training to non-technical audiences on poverty analytics.
