Research Portfolio

Five papers, one connected argument about UK housing, rent regulation, and the welfare of working households. Each abstract can be read in under a minute and links to the full paper.

Reading order

If you want the shortest route from policy argument to evidence to theory:

  1. Breaking the Benchmark — why the standard anti-rent-control objection fails in the actual UK PRS.
  2. Fiscal Capture — quantitative evidence that LHA support is partly absorbed by landlords.
  3. Rent as Reproduction Cost — welfare rationale for a CPI-anchored emergency brake.
  4. Labor Power as Numéraire — the underlying welfare foundation.
  5. A Progressing Problemshift — a transparent self-audit of what is established, conditional, and open.

1. Breaking the Benchmark: Emergency Rent Regulation in a Financialised UK Housing Market

Full paper: ../working-papers/breaking-the-benchmark.html

What it does: Defends NEF’s emergency rent brake by showing the standard textbook objection to rent control does not apply to the UK market. Uses Lipsey-Lancaster second-best logic to show that once competitive conditions fail on multiple fronts (planning constraints, financialisation, credit conditions), deregulation loses its automatic-efficiency claim. Explains the two critical design features: no vacancy decontrol, and pre-committed statutory exit criteria.

For NEF: Policy justification with economic rigour. Shows why the brake is the right first move, and on what conditions it should end.

2. Fiscal Capture in the UK Private Rented Sector: Evidence from Two LHA Policy Events

Full paper: ../working-papers/fiscal-capture.html

What it does: Quasi-experimental evidence using two natural policy events (April 2020 LHA uplift, April 2021 re-freeze) across 290 local authorities. Finds consistent evidence of landlord capture: every coefficient negative, zero sign reversals across 26 specifications. Reports headline figure as conservative lower bound. Names the distinguishing demographic test still to run.

For NEF: Hard quantitative evidence that benefit increases partly flow to landlords unless paired with a rent ceiling. Empirical anchor for why benefits and regulation are complements.

3. Labor Power as Numéraire: A Materialist Theory of Value, Risk, and Capitalist Finance

Full paper: ../working-papers/labor-power-numeraire.html

What it does: Proposes Labour-Power Dominance as the welfare criterion for evaluating economic outcomes. Shows that competitive markets systematically fail this criterion when initial wealth is unequal, even with perfect competition and complete information. Derives testable implications for asset pricing and macrofinance.

For NEF: Welfare foundation. Explains why markets fail to sustain the working population, and why that failure is structural and measurable, not an accident.

4. Rent as Reproduction Cost: Labor-Power Dynamics, Buy-to-Let Mispricing, and Fiscal Capture in the UK Private Rented Sector

Full paper: ../working-papers/rent-reproduction.html

What it does: Treats housing cost as an input to labour-power reproduction, not consumption. Derives a welfare-optimal rent ceiling consistent with household continuation. Conditional on post-2008 buy-to-let overvaluation, market rents exceed the ceiling. CPI-anchored cap moves the market back toward the optimal path.

For NEF: Welfare rationale for the emergency brake. Not just a second-best compromise—under the reproduction framework, the brake is the more efficient choice.

5. A Progressing Problemshift: A Lakatosian Rational Reconstruction of the Labour-Power-as-Numéraire Research Programme

Full paper: ../working-papers/progressing-problemshift.html

What it does: Appraises the four-paper programme using Imre Lakatos’s philosophy-of-science framework. Asks: under criticism, did the programme add genuine testable content, or patch with ad hoc fixes? Split verdict: theoretically progressive, non-degenerating, but empirically progressing not yet progressive. The discriminating test is named and unrun.

For NEF: Transparency about rigour and limits. Shows the research programme is built to be checked, not to intimidate. Flags where evidence stops and future work begins.

Where to start: - Read only the first three if you want the shortest route from policy argument to evidence to welfare rationale. - Read the fourth and fifth to inspect the deeper theoretical foundation and the programme’s own self-critique. - Follow the full-paper links when an abstract raises a question you want answered in detail.
