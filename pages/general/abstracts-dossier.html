Research Portfolio

Five papers, one connected argument about UK housing, rent regulation, and the welfare of working households. Each entry gives the claim in plain English, what it contributes to the policy case, and what is technically inside — with links to both the short explainer and the full paper.

Before the papers: what the research is actually for — Manifesto.

Suggested path: read top to bottom — the evidence, the policy case, the welfare rationale, the foundation, and the self-audit.

Fiscal Capture — empirical anchor

Quantitative evidence that much of housing benefit is absorbed by landlords rather than tenants.

Policy use: any rise in housing support has to be paired with rent regulation, or it leaks into landlord yields instead of reaching the households it was meant for.

Inside: difference-in-differences across 290 local authorities over two opposite LHA events (the April 2020 uplift and the April 2021 re-freeze); 26 specifications, every coefficient negative, zero sign reversals; the headline effect is reported as a conservative lower bound.

Read the explainer · Read the full paper

Why the Rent-Control Veto Fails — clearing the ground for regulation

Why the textbook “rent control chokes off supply” objection fails in the actual UK market.

Policy use: the rebuttal to the standard veto on rent regulation — it defends an emergency rent brake, with no vacancy decontrol and pre-committed statutory exit criteria.

Inside: models the private rented sector as a quadruple-object market (shelter, capitalised asset, credit collateral, fiscal conduit); applies Lipsey–Lancaster second-best logic.

Read the explainer · Read the full paper

Rent as Reproduction Cost — welfare rationale

Treats rent as a cost of sustaining the working population across generations, not as ordinary consumption.

Policy use: a principled basis for where to set the cap — pegged to reproduction cost (CPI-anchored), not to financialised market rents.

Inside: derives a welfare-optimal rent ceiling consistent with household continuation; conditional on post-2008 buy-to-let overvaluation, market rents exceed it; the result is a scoped first-order perturbation, not a general law.

Read the explainer · Read the full paper

Labor Power as Numéraire — foundation

The underlying value theory: why competitive markets can systematically fail the people whose labour sustains the economy.

What it secures: the welfare foundation the other arguments rest on — it makes the case structural rather than a value judgment opponents can wave away. Co-authored (Huato and Galindo).

Inside: proposes Labour-Power Dominance as a welfare criterion; shows competitive equilibria fail it under unequal initial wealth even with complete information; derives implications for asset pricing and macro-finance.

Read the explainer · Read the full paper

A Progressing Problemshift — self-audit

A transparent appraisal of the whole body of work: what is established, what is conditional, and what is still open.

What it secures: trustworthy advocacy — it names the one discriminating test still unrun, so the case cannot be ambushed on a hidden weakness.

Inside: a Lakatosian reconstruction of the research programme; the verdict is split and stated plainly — theoretically progressive and non-degenerating, but empirically progressing, not yet progressive, because the discriminating demographic test is named but unrun.
