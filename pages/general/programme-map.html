The Programme in One Diagram

Every paper here was built backwards from one question: what would it take — proven, and able to survive the strongest attack that can be mounted against it — to justify a single intervention in Britain’s housing market? The result is a policy case with a clear spine: the usual objection to rent regulation does not hold in this market; the welfare argument and the evidence both point the same way; and one question is left honestly open. Here is the whole case on one screen. Click any box to read the paper behind it.

The case, in plain terms

Why the usual objection fails. The block on rent regulation is a single economic claim used as a conversation-stopper — rent controls choke supply and harm the people they protect. Why the Rent-Control Veto Fails shows it does not hold here: Britain’s rented sector has failed the competitive conditions that claim assumes since 1988, so the standard presumption against a brake falls away. This clears the ground; it does not yet make the case.

The case for action. Rent as Reproduction Cost sets a welfare ceiling on rent — pegged to what it costs to sustain a household across generations, not to financialised market values — and argues market rents now exceed it. It rests on the value theory of Labor Power as Numéraire, developed with Julio Huato and prior to the housing question, not bent to fit it. Fiscal Capture supplies the evidence that the harm is real: across 290 local authorities and more than twenty specifications, landlords absorb much of the housing subsidy, with no sign reversals.

The policy — and the open question. Together these support an emergency rent brake, paired with supply expansion and bound to pre-committed exit criteria. A Progressing Problemshift then sets out plainly what is settled and what is not — and names the one question that would decide it against the orthodox reading: whether sustained rent pressure suppresses household formation. That question has not yet been tested. Naming it first is how you keep hold of the argument when a reader goes looking for the weak point on your behalf.
