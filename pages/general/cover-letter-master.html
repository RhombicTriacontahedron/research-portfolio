Extended Cover Letter (Full Glory)

Carlos Galindo Escajeda · cgalindito@gmail.com · London, UK · +44 (0) 737-677-1146 Eligible to work in the UK

Dear Recruitment Team,

Reading NEF’s A Modern System for Fair Rents set me reading, then mulling, then following one idea into the next — and that is the honest origin of this application for the Social Policy Economist / Quantitative Researcher position. I did not begin with a grand design, least of all a plan to “correct” NEF. It was closer to self-discovery under a hard time limit: the questions I kept pulling on organically coalesced into a five-paper research programme, and the deeper I dug, the more independent lines of inquiry converged — not on whether to intervene in the UK private rented sector, but on a single genuinely contentious question that I did not go looking for: the durability of the intervention, and how far the case for an emergency brake actually extends toward a permanent fair-rents regime. What follows is who built that, how, and the spirit in which I would want to do it from inside your team.

That kind of from-scratch sprint is not new for me — it is how I have worked since long before AI existed, and I have real confidence in it. My first serious research ran exactly this pattern. I was working on poverty and demographic risk in a context very different from the UK — Mexico’s low-income household sector — but the policy question was structurally similar to what NEF works on: how do we distribute the burden and benefit of social risk across the income distribution? That puzzle led me to microsimulate poverty-reduction policy. To do it, I had to teach myself three unfamiliar things at once, from a standing start: how relational databases actually work (well outside the clean tabular data of an econometrics course); how to handle the complex sampling design of SEDESOL’s household survey microdata — researching its structure and the processing gymnastics it demands until I could manipulate and explain it fluidly; and how the Ravallion microsimulation literature operated. I did it in under six months, while finishing my master’s degree and writing a thesis on an entirely separate subject — a two-sector overlapping-generations model of household heterogeneity, growth, and convergence between rich and poor — and I learned to program in STATA for the microsimulation work specifically. That poverty work became peer-reviewed publications on poverty measurement and policy simulation. Then I did the part I am proudest of: I packaged the hard-won processing knowledge and gave it away, running 700-plus hours of STATA training so that undergraduates could finally mine survey microdata that had gone under-exploited precisely because it was so hard to process — feeding a stream of undergraduate theses. Multiplying the people around me, not only shipping my own output, is the disposition I would bring to your team.

From there I spent the better part of a decade building quantitative models in macro-finance — emerging-market forecasting, Bayesian time-series and DSGE work, even reverse-engineering parts of the Federal Reserve’s FRB/US model to price scenarios the Fed and the market had missed — at Deutsche Bank, Bank of America, and a global macro fund. Applying to your social-policy team is, for me, a deliberate return to where I began, now with a macro modeller’s toolkit behind it.

So read my recent arrival in UK housing policy the right way. Until I saw this posting I was a tourist in it — but “tourist” is how nearly every one of my best projects has begun; it names how I work, not a ceiling on where I reach. Two honest caveats follow.

First: do not read the theoretical papers as me wanting to live in abstraction. I am a firm believer in transforming material reality — that is what drew me to policy in the first place. But I also hold theory and empirical science in high esteem, not as substitutes but as complements: theory disciplines what we ask, empirics answers it, and the two together correct each other. You will find the theoretical papers are about hardening the welfare case for policies NEF already advocates for, grounding them in structural arguments that survive hostile scrutiny. I am not here to replace policy expertise with theory; I am here to bring rigour as a shield.

If this paragraph is the thing that worries you, I have written it out in full — what the research programme is actually for, why each paper contributes to a specific intervention rather than to the literature, and why theory and action are not in tension in this work: Manifesto.

Second: please do not expect me to recite every line flawlessly across an interview table. After a sprint this broad and this fast, total recall would be neither realistic nor necessary. What matters is that the work is verifiable on the page — and that it is genuinely original and non-trivial, by orders of magnitude, not a repackaging of the familiar. I can rebuild any part of it on request, and that ability to reconstruct from first principles, rather than to recite, is the capability I would actually bring to your team.

A brief note on method. The five papers were not generated by automation; they were built under a verification-first discipline that I would bring to NEF: every load-bearing claim is either verified against a primary source, survived logged adversarial challenge, or explicitly flagged open. The best evidence that this is real and not rhetorical is the fifth paper, A Progressing Problemshift: it applies mainstream philosophy-of-science standards to my own programme and, against my own immediate advantage, downgrades the strongest-looking verdict from “empirically progressive” to “empirically progressing” because the discriminating test is named but still unrun. That is the reflex I would bring to your team under external scrutiny: ambitious claims, strict boundaries, and transparent uncertainty where evidence has not yet closed the question. If it helps to see the workbench rather than take my word for it, this short screen-capture shows that method in motion — human-on-the-loop, with AI held on a tight, audited leash. It is the visible tip of a far larger body of by-hand mathematics, econometrics, and manual data work.

The programme maps directly onto your specification.

Empirical rigour on large UK datasets. Fiscal Capture in the UK Private Rented Sector is independent, critical panel-data work: a difference-in-differences design across 290 English local authorities (2019–2024), exploiting the April 2020 LHA uplift and April 2021 re-freeze as directionally opposite policy events. Across 26 specifications the signature is consistent with landlord capture, with zero sign reversals — and I report the headline coefficient as a deliberate conservative lower bound, naming the attenuation channels (downward nominal rigidity, tenant lock-in) rather than inflating it.

Challenging orthodoxy without overreach. Breaking the Benchmark dismantles the “price controls choke off supply” veto by modelling the UK PRS as a structurally distorted quadruple-object market — shelter, capitalised asset, credit collateral, and fiscal conduit at once. Lipsey–Lancaster second-best logic then cuts symmetrically: once the competitive conditions are absent, deregulation forfeits its automatic-efficiency claim exactly as much as regulation does. The paper defends your emergency brake and hardens it — no vacancy decontrol, plus pre-committed statutory exit criteria (sustained ~300,000 net completions, a bottom-quintile cost-burden threshold) that bind it to objective milestones rather than open-ended discretion.

A welfare foundation aligned with your mission. Building on the materialist value theory Julio Huato develops in his book and in our co-authored paper, Labor Power as Numéraire, I derived a structural companion — Rent as Reproduction Cost — that grounds the argument in the physical reproduction of the working population rather than subjective utility. It reframes high rents as a structural deduction from households’ capacity to sustain and form themselves across generations, turning a CPI-anchored cap from an affordability patch into a structural correction with a coherent welfare rationale.

The five abstracts — one for each paper — are collected in the Research Portfolio. If you only wanted the fastest route through the research, I would suggest starting with Breaking the Benchmark, then Fiscal Capture, then Rent as Reproduction Cost. Nothing in this letter depends on your opening them; they are there only if you would like the shape of each argument in a minute rather than a full manuscript.

If you prefer a role-mapped profile snapshot before the full research narrative, use the Short CV (and Full Glory CV for complete detail).

The one place this process led me into genuinely contentious territory is exactly that durability question. The second-best argument disarms the orthodox veto robustly for the temporary emergency brake — but extending the case to a permanent fair-rents regime is the harder, more contested step, because the emergency rationale lapses as the underlying distortions are addressed, and a durable regime therefore needs a different and more demanding foundation than the brake itself supplies. I did not set out to find that fault line; multiple independent roads led me to it. I flag it not to lecture but because locating precisely where an argument becomes contestable is the most useful thing a defender can do — and because it is the first of many nuances on which I expect your experience to correct and deepen mine.

That is the spirit in which I apply. I am not here to challenge you and become a pest; I am here to show that I bring a tremendous appetite for learning and that I can demonstrably deliver. I do want to learn from the experts, however — which is precisely why I am applying for a relatively junior role: to understand, over time and from seasoned colleagues, the multitude of institutional, political, and practical nuances that my preliminary work almost certainly underestimates. I am willing to learn and to listen. I am not here to challenge your ontological position — I agree with it fully. I believe I can help strengthen it, especially against the most furious attacks from the most conservative and powerful interests: a quantitative shield, not a sword aimed inward.

My toolkit (MATLAB/DYNARE, STATA, Python, R, EViews) and my capacity for rapid, verified delivery are ready to support NEF’s research priorities immediately. I have already identified the next empirical gaps — ground-rent-share estimation and transaction-level search-friction markups — that NEF’s proposed PRS-database expansion is positioned to close. The verification-first discipline reduces institutional risk; the demonstrated delivery on the housing programme adds pace; and the alignment with your mission plus the genuine appetite for learning signal both capability and long-term fit.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how I can support NEF’s vital mission.

Sincerely,

Carlos Galindo Escajeda
