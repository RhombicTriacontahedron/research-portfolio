Labor Power as Numeraire

Capitalist Finance Julio Huato & Carlos Galindo Escajeda 2026-05-01

Abstract

This foundational paper supplies the welfare grammar the rest of the programme draws on, and aligns it with NEF’s commitment to an economy organised around people rather than asset wealth. Mainstream macro-finance evaluates outcomes by subjective utility and Pareto efficiency — standards under which market-generated inequality can register as “efficient.” This paper replaces that with Labor-Power Dominance (LPD): a criterion that ranks economic paths by the physical reproduction of the working population over time. Using the same dynamic-stochastic (Bellman) machinery mainstream economists use to argue for market efficiency, it shows that even under idealised, frictionless complete markets, competitive institutions can fail this criterion once initial capital is unequally held — and that a structural “demographic continuation” channel makes today’s misallocation compound into future deficits in the population’s capacity to reproduce itself. For NEF this is the heterodox anchor: a formal argument, conducted on orthodox economics’ own terms, that unregulated capitalist finance can systematically erode human reproduction, and so calls for exactly the structural, redistributive interventions NEF advances. Its risk-premium theorem is presented as a conditional second-order local perturbation result, not a global exact claim. The paper is explicit that LPD is a normative choice, openly defended against the utilitarian default rather than smuggled in as neutral, and that its results hold within a stationary modelling frame — the honest boundary of an immanent critique.

JEL Classification: C61, D50, E11, G12, P10

Keywords: labor-power reproduction, materialist macro-finance, demographic continuation term, stochastic discount factor, Labor-Power Dominance, heterodox asset pricing, materialist growth and finance

1. Introduction

Conventional economic theory takes subjective utility as its primitive. This paper takes a different ontological starting point. We posit that society’s proper objective is the maximization of an objective physical index of labor-power reproduction — denoted \(N\) — governed by a reproduction technology with constant returns to scale:

\[ N_{t+1} = B e^{\eta_t} N_t^{1-\gamma} C_t^\gamma. \]

Throughout, \(C_t\) is interpreted as reproduction-effective goods and services input (rather than raw expenditure units), so the CRS map above is read as a physical transformation rule in effective units.

Labor power is the fundamental numéraire in which all economic values must ultimately be expressed. This numéraire is not an arbitrary welfare metric: it is the formal expression of the claim that the intra-temporal reproduction of living labor and its inter-temporal comparability are jointly constitutive of value, money, capital, and fictitious capital. This joint necessity — which Huato (2026, p. 32) identifies as the foundation of the entire value-theoretic edifice — entails that asset valuation must be derived from the same reproductive process rather than appended to it as an external financial convenience.

This paper’s approach to materialist macro-finance is distinct from prior heterodox asset-pricing attempts. Harvey (1982, 2010), Hilferding (1981), Haila (2016), and Durand (2017) analyze fictitious capital and rent as expressions of the capital-relation but operate without a formal dynamic model. Foley (1982) and the SFRA tradition price financial assets through circuit-of-capital accounting rather than a stochastic discount factor.1 Huato (2026) develops the conceptual framework most directly antecedent to the present paper: it argues that the allocation of social labor and the valuation of resulting wealth constitute a duality, that inter-temporal comparability of labor is a trans-historical material presupposition of any reproducing society, and that the monetary interest rate has a determinate material anchor in this inter-temporal valuation — claims whose formal Bellman derivation constitutes the present paper’s core contribution.2 Throughout this paper, “fictitious capital” is used in Huato’s (2026) broader yield-hierarchy sense — an asset whose price capitalizes a ranked structure of claims on future social labor through the conditions of reproduction — rather than only in Marx’s narrower Vol.~III sense of the capitalization of surplus-value streams. The paper addresses the theoretical gap Huato (2026) traces in modern finance theory: the absence of commodified labor power and of the institutions of capitalist reproduction from the foundations of asset pricing. Its novelty lies in deriving asset prices as direct outputs of the materialist Bellman problem — not as external accounting identities — as established in Appendix A.2. The demographic continuation term \((1-\gamma)\) has no counterpart in any of these traditions.

The circuit-of-capital approach initiated by Foley (1982) and developed within the SFRA tradition tracks value through its metamorphoses — money capital, productive capital, commodity capital — and prices financial claims as proportional shares of future surplus-value flows. This framework captures the institutional form of financial circuits with a precision neoclassical asset pricing cannot approach. Its formal limit, however, is structural: circuit accounting traverses discrete period-by-period value equations; it does not produce an intertemporal Bellman recursion or a pricing kernel whose innovation variance generates covariance-based risk premiums. The cross-equation restriction \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\) — the central testable output of the present paper — requires the second-order perturbation of a stochastic Bellman operator, not a circuit equation. The paper situates itself here not to displace circuit-accounting analysis but to open a complementary terrain: the formal derivation of the risk premium from within the recursive grammar that competitive equilibrium theory uses to establish its own efficiency claims.

Placed against the broader heterodox canon, the paper engages three adjacent literatures of importance to a CJE readership. Panico (1988) reconstructs the relation between interest and profit inside value-and-distribution theory, providing a classical benchmark from which the present Bellman-derived interest rate departs by incorporating demographic reproduction into the pricing kernel. Itoh and Lapavitsas (1998) treat money and finance as constitutive moments of capitalist political economy rather than an auxiliary veil — the institutional terrain on which the present asset-pricing formalization is situated. Shaikh (2016) supplies the real-competition and national-accounts context that any empirical extension must engage, in particular the problem of relating the paper’s structurally constrained effective risk price to profit-rate equalization and aggregate-accounting decompositions; reconciling that terrain with the present Bellman closure requires further theoretical work and does not amount to importing Shaikh’s ontology wholesale.

Although the paper is theoretical, it already yields a concrete empirical restriction. The effective risk price is structurally constrained — not freely calibrated — because the cross-equation relation \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\) ties it to reproduction elasticity, the capital-ratio savings response, the normalized-consumption response coefficient, and Bellman-curvature objects. The natural empirical follow-up is therefore not free calibration but testing that restriction against observable profit rates, risk-free rates, wage shares, accumulation paths, cohort fertility data, investment-share dynamics, and the response of normalized consumption to identified technology shocks.

Prior formalizations of this materialist hard core have incorporated three internally contradictory devices:

  • Stochastic formulations that drop the \(N_t^{1-\gamma}\) term, violating constant returns to scale.
  • Welfare claims based on Pareto efficiency and the first welfare theorem.
  • Comparative statics imported from subjective utility models rather than derived from physical \(N\)-variance.

The first contradiction has the deepest structural consequences: without \(N_t^{1-\gamma}\), joint CRS fails, the value function admits no exact normalization, the Bellman perturbation yields no closed-form risk premium, and the LPD violation has no structural coefficient to sign-identify. These contradictions are terminal. This paper resolves them by correcting the model’s auxiliary structure. The formalization satisfies three requirements:

  1. Full consistency with the CRS reproduction technology, including the demographic continuation term.
  2. Internal derivation of all asset pricing results.
  3. Replacement of Pareto efficiency with Labor-Power Dominance.

This paper develops a materialist macro-finance framework centered on the reproduction of labor power. Its formal strategy runs from the competitive equilibrium side: rather than starting from a planner’s benchmark and measuring distortions from the optimum, it asks what the competitive equilibrium proves about itself from its own first-order conditions. It opens a broader research agenda on growth, finance, and social reproduction without claiming to settle the full historical questions usually associated with unified growth theory in a single paper.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 develops the materialist stochastic baseline — the Bellman problem with \(N\) as the primitive objective and the constant-returns reproduction technology \(N_{t+1} = Be^{\eta_t}N_t^{1-\gamma}C_t^\gamma\) — and derives the structural Euler equation with the demographic continuation term \((1-\gamma)\) explicit, together with the stochastic discount factor \(m_{t+1} = \beta\lambda_{t+1}/\lambda_t\). The closed-form reduction of the Euler equation and the derivation of \(\Omega\) are the subject of Appendix A.2. Section 3 introduces Labor-Power Dominance: allocations are ranked directly over discounted dynasty-level \(N\)-paths, and Proposition 3.1 establishes that initial capital inequality generates persistent violations, compounded by \((1-\gamma)\). Section 4 states Theorem 4.1 as a conditional second-order local perturbation result for the materialist risk premium, with the demographic coefficient \(\Omega(\sigma,\gamma,\beta)\) sign-identified under two standard curvature and savings-rate conditions. Section 5 examines LPD violations under autarky, credit markets, and labor markets. Section 6 introduces incomplete markets and the precautionary saving channel through which idiosyncratic risk deepens reproductive deficits; it also shows how the derived SDF prices a yield hierarchy in which capitalist wealth takes the form of fictitious capital within the model’s asset span — the class-portfolio generalization across heterogeneous asset classes and endogenous credit is deferred to future work. Appendix A.2 applies the Schmitt-Grohé/Uribe second-order perturbation to derive Theorem 4.1.

2. The Materialist Stochastic Baseline

Production and reproduction are subject to multiplicative shocks:

\[ Y_t = A e^{\theta_t} N_t^{1-\alpha} K_t^\alpha, \quad N_{t+1} = B e^{\eta_t} N_t^{1-\gamma} C_t^\gamma. \]

The capital accumulation equation is \(K_{t+1} = Y_t - C_t + (1-\delta)K_t\). The Bellman equation is:

\[ V(N_t, K_t) = \max_{C_t} \Bigl\{ u(N_t) + \beta \mathbb{E}_t \bigl[ V(N_{t+1}, K_{t+1}) \bigr] \Bigr\}. \]

Timing convention: The shocks \((\eta_t, \theta_t)\) are realized at the beginning of period \(t\) and are part of the period-\(t\) information set \(\mathcal{F}_t\), before the consumption choice \(C_t\) is made. Consequently \(N_{t+1}\) and \(K_{t+1}\) are \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable, as is \(V_K(N_{t+1},K_{t+1})\). The shadow value \(\lambda_t \equiv \beta\mathbb{E}_t[V_K(N_{t+1},K_{t+1})] = \beta V_K(N_{t+1},K_{t+1})\) is therefore \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable — it is known at \(t\). By contrast, \(\lambda_{t+1} \equiv \beta V_K(N_{t+2},K_{t+2})\) is \(\mathcal{F}_{t+1}\)-measurable and genuinely uncertain at \(t\), because \(N_{t+2}\) and \(K_{t+2}\) depend on \((\eta_{t+1},\theta_{t+1}) \notin \mathcal{F}_t\) through the next-period reproduction and capital laws of motion. The SDF \(m_{t+1} = \beta\lambda_{t+1}/\lambda_t = \beta V_K(N_{t+2},K_{t+2})/V_K(N_{t+1},K_{t+1})\) is therefore \(\mathcal{F}_{t+1}\)-measurable and genuinely stochastic from the time-\(t\) perspective, making non-zero covariance with risky returns possible. The value function \(V(N_t,K_t)\) denotes the Bellman value after absorbing period-\(t\) shocks; the dependence on \((\eta_t,\theta_t)\) is implicit.

We specify \(u(N) = \frac{N^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma}\) with \(\sigma > 0\). The functional form is formally identical to standard CRRA utility; the structural departure does not lie in the apparatus but in two specific places: (i) the state variable is the physical reproductive stock \(N\) rather than subjective consumption \(C\), which alters the policy function and produces distinct empirical implications for asset prices; and (ii) the demographic continuation term \((1-\gamma)\) appears in the Euler equation — a term with no counterpart in any standard Ramsey formulation — through which reproductive persistence alters risk premia. The parameter \(\sigma\) governs the planner’s attitude toward variance in aggregate reproductive capacity.3

The Bellman flow payoff \(u(N_t)\) is defined over the current-period reproductive stock \(N_t\) — the realized population at the start of period \(t\), determined by last period’s choice of \(C_{t-1}\). This timing convention reflects the interpretation that society derives period-\(t\) productive capacity from the existing labor force \(N_t\); the current choice \(C_t\) governs next period’s reproductive level \(N_{t+1}\).

First-Order Condition and Envelope Conditions

The first-order condition with respect to consumption \(C_t\) defines the shadow value of capital \(\lambda_t\):4

\[ \lambda_t \equiv \beta \mathbb{E}_t [ V_K(N_{t+1}, K_{t+1}) ] = \beta \mathbb{E}_t \Bigl[ V_N(N_{t+1}, K_{t+1}) \frac{\partial N_{t+1}}{\partial C_t} \Bigr] = \beta \mathbb{E}_t \Bigl[ V_N(N_{t+1}, K_{t+1}) \cdot \gamma \frac{N_{t+1}}{C_t} \Bigr]. \]

The envelope condition for capital \(K_t\) isolates the marginal product of capital, which is independent of demographic terms:

\[ V_K(N_t, K_t) = \beta \mathbb{E}_t \Bigl[ V_K(N_{t+1}, K_{t+1}) \Bigl( \alpha \frac{Y_t}{K_t} + 1 - \delta \Bigr) \Bigr] = \lambda_t R_{K,t}. \]

The envelope condition for labor power \(N_t\) accounts for both its role in production (wage \(w_t\)) and reproduction:

\[ V_N(N_t, K_t) = u'(N_t) + \lambda_t w_t + \beta \mathbb{E}_t \Bigl[ V_N(N_{t+1}, K_{t+1}) (1-\gamma) \frac{N_{t+1}}{N_t} \Bigr]. \]

To resolve dimensional consistency across the two state variables, note that \(w_t\) is the physical marginal product of labor (goods per unit of \(N\)). The shadow-price-deflated real wage — the marginal rate at which the planner can trade capital accumulation for reproductive capacity at the margin, scaled by the physical wage — is therefore \(w_t^* = w_t \cdot \frac{\lambda_t}{u'(N_t)}\).

The Structural Euler Equation (open form)

Iterating the \(V_N\) envelope condition one period forward and substituting it into the first-order condition for \(C_t\) yields the following necessary condition:

\[ \lambda_t = \beta \mathbb{E}_t \Biggl[ \gamma \frac{N_{t+1}}{C_t} \Bigl( u'(N_{t+1}) + \lambda_{t+1} w_{t+1} + \beta \mathbb{E}_{t+1} \Bigl[ V_N(N_{t+2}, K_{t+2}) \cdot (1-\gamma) \frac{N_{t+2}}{N_{t+1}} \Bigr] \Bigr) \Biggr]. \]

This structural condition is open-form: \(V_N\) appears on the right-hand side and generates an infinite-horizon recursion whose closed-form reduction is established in Appendix A.2 (through the homogeneous value function \(V = N^{1-\sigma}\Psi(K/N)\) and the Schmitt-Grohé/Uribe second-order perturbation). The closed-form Euler equation and derived risk premium are established in Theorem 4.1 and Appendix A.2. The final term \(\beta\mathbb{E}_{t+1}[\dots]\) is the demographic continuation term — it generates a structural channel through which capital misallocation today compounds non-linearly into future labor-power deficits. Unlike the dynastic-altruism terms in Barro-Becker endogenous-fertility models, it cannot be calibrated away: it is a consequence of the CRS reproductive technology parameter \(\gamma\), not of parental preference. The positive reproduction elasticity \(\gamma\) is a physical parameter governing the aggregate resource intensity of labor-power reproduction under constant returns, not a reduced-form fertility preference parameter; observed income-fertility gradients therefore lie outside the model’s specified domain. Its magnitude is pinned by the same technology that governs \(\Omega\) in Theorem 4.1.

Stochastic Discount Factor

\[ m_{t+1} = \beta \frac{\lambda_{t+1}}{\lambda_t}. \]

This expression follows from the definition \(\lambda_t \equiv \beta\mathbb{E}_t[V_K(N_{t+1},K_{t+1})]\) and the standard pricing kernel \(\mathbb{E}_t[m_{t+1}R_{t+1}]=1\); the closed-form expression for \(m_{t+1}\) in terms of observables is established in Appendix A.2. The derivation is not merely a formality: it reveals that the SDF innovation contains a demographic continuation channel — the \(\psi_x\hat{x}^{\text{innov}}_{t+2}\) term derived in Appendix A.2 — that carries the \((1-\gamma)\) reproductive persistence into the pricing kernel and is entirely absent from any a priori declared SDF form; this channel is the structural basis of Theorem 4.1’s departure from standard CCAPM.5

3. Labor-Power Dominance — A Native Welfare Criterion

Institutionally, the criterion developed below is meant to capture the standpoint of a planner or collective reproduction authority charged with preserving the material reproduction of the laboring population across dynasties. From that standpoint, variance matters insofar as it lowers expected reproductive capacity or entrenches class-differentiated deficits, not as an independent source of disutility.

Definition (Labor-Power Dominance)
Allocation \(A\) Labor-Power Dominates allocation \(B\) under a discounted variant of the Ramsey-Weizsäcker overtaking criterion — replacing undiscounted partial sums with \(\beta^t\)-weighted partial sums — if (i) for every dynasty \(i\), \(\liminf_{T \to \infty} \mathbb{E}_0\left[\sum_{t=0}^T \beta^t (N_{i,t}^A - N_{i,t}^B)\right] \ge 0\), and (ii) for at least one dynasty the inequality is strictly positive. The criterion is adopted precisely because \(N_t\) grows along the equilibrium path and the discounted absolute sum may not converge; no additional spectral bound is imposed. The Bellman transversality condition \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\beta^T \mathbb{E}[V_K K_T] = 0\) is a separate requirement on the optimum.

Remark (LPD structure and Paretian comparison). The classical Ramsey-Weizsäcker criterion uses undiscounted partial sums; the \(\beta^t\)-weighting here preserves the overtaking structure — ranking by liminf of finite partial sums without requiring global convergence — while maintaining commensurability with the dynastic Bellman objective. The discounted overtaking criterion is a partial order: it is not transitive in general; transitivity holds on the restricted class of paths where the liminf converges, which is the relevant case for bounded stochastic equilibria. The axiomatics of LPD relative to Paretian and utilitarian orderings is deferred to future work. As a preliminary comparison: LPD is structurally linear (risk-neutral) in expected \(N_{i,t}\), whereas a standard Paretian objective over dynasty welfare is concave (risk-averse) in \(N_{i,t}\) for \(\sigma > 0\). A mean-preserving spread over \(N\)-paths therefore leaves LPD indifferent while strictly degrading Paretian welfare; LPD can rank an allocation as dominant that a Paretian welfare function would reject, and vice versa. This divergence is a feature: LPD evaluates the expected physical reproductive capacity of each dynasty directly, without penalizing variance in \(N_t\) as such, precisely because reproductive capacity is treated as an objective quantity rather than a utility-bearing argument. The criterion measures capitalist failure on capitalism’s own terrain — the reproduction of commodified labor power — which is precisely the terrain on which the capitalist yield hierarchy implicitly evaluates reproductive outcomes. That this terrain excludes ecological throughput, care-work capacity, and non-wage reproductive dimensions is a precision, not a limitation: a broader materialist welfare criterion incorporating those dimensions is the subject of future work, but it would require a different formal object; the present analysis is complete within its deliberately scoped domain.

Dynasty-extension model. Propositions 3.1, 5.1, and 6.1 apply to a model extension in which a finite set of dynasties \(i \in \{1, \ldots, I\}\) share the production and reproduction technology with identical parameters but heterogeneous initial capital endowments \(K_{i,0}\). Each dynasty \(i\) independently maximizes \(\mathbb{E}_0\left[\sum_{t=0}^\infty \beta^t \frac{N_{i,t}^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma}\right]\) taking aggregate prices as given. For the price-taking assumption to be internally consistent under persistent \(x_i\)-heterogeneity, all dynasty propositions are understood in the large-\(I\) continuum limit in which each dynasty is atomistic (\(N_{i,t}/\sum_j N_{j,t}\to 0\)) and individual decisions have no measurable effect on the aggregate rental rate \(R_{K,t}\) or wage \(w_t\); finite-\(I\) general-equilibrium feedback from persistent \(N_i\)-gaps on aggregate factor prices is left for future work. A further implication of the atomistic closure is that as \(N\)-ratios diverge multiplicatively under Proposition 3.1, the wealthiest dynasties eventually command non-atomistic shares of aggregate labor power — at which point the yield hierarchy becomes strategic rather than parametric, a structural result that constitutes, within DSGE syntax, a formal derivation of the concentration-and-centralization tendency: competitive institutions do not merely permit but necessitate the erosion of their own behavioral foundation. The strategic analysis of the resulting non-atomistic yield hierarchy — where the wealthiest dynasties hold pricing power over factor markets — is the subject of the finite-\(I\) extension. Production is organized by a competitive representative firm that hires the aggregate factor supplies \(N_t = \sum_i N_{i,t}\) and \(K_t = \sum_i K_{i,t}\), so aggregate output is \(Y_t = Ae^{\theta_t}N_t^{1-\alpha}K_t^\alpha\) and dynasties are factor suppliers rather than dynasty-specific production units. Competitive factor markets clear at: \(w_t = (1-\alpha)Y_t/N_t\) (wage equals marginal product of labor power) and \(R_{K,t} = \alpha Y_t/K_t + 1 - \delta\) (rental rate equals marginal product of capital). The aggregate resource constraint is \(\sum_i C_{i,t} + K_{t+1} = Y_t + (1-\delta)K_t\). Under complete markets, this competitive equilibrium coincides with the social planner’s optimum by the first welfare theorem applied to the \(N\)-primitive objective (the theorem requires only competitive prices and convex feasibility sets, which are satisfied here); the LPD violations established below therefore arise within the space of Pareto-efficient competitive equilibria — comparing unequal-endowment and equal-endowment CEs that the Paretian ordering cannot rank, so LPD supplies the discriminating criterion. In the complete-markets benchmark, dynasties trade the full set of state-contingent claims for idiosyncratic shocks \(\eta_{i,t}\); this is the maintained asset span for Propositions 3.1 and 5.1 unless otherwise stated. Dynasties do not trade claims on aggregate shocks \((\theta_t, \eta_t)\); aggregate risk is borne through the common rental rate \(R_{K,t}\) and is not diversifiable at the dynasty level — this rules out GE price externalities from aggregate-shock insurance that could otherwise dampen or reverse the \((1-\gamma)\) persistence channel. Incomplete markets (6) must specify the available asset span explicitly.

Definition (Competitive Equilibrium). A competitive equilibrium is a sequence of prices \(\{w_t, R_{K,t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}\) and allocations \(\{C_{i,t}, K_{i,t+1}\}_{i,t}\) such that: (i) each dynasty \(i\) solves its individual Bellman problem taking prices as given; (ii) factor markets clear: \(w_t = (1-\alpha)Y_t/N_t\) and \(R_{K,t} = \alpha Y_t/K_t + 1-\delta\); (iii) the aggregate resource constraint holds: \(\sum_i C_{i,t} + K_{t+1} = Y_t + (1-\delta)K_t\); and (iv) the transversality condition \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\beta^T\mathbb{E}[V_K(N_{i,T},K_{i,T})\,K_{i,T}] = 0\) holds for each dynasty \(i\).

Assumption (A3) (Monotone Policy Function): The optimal normalized consumption policy \(\tilde{c}(x, z)\) of each dynasty is strictly increasing in the own normalized capital ratio \(x = K/N\) for all aggregate state \(z\). The exact sufficient condition on the marginal propensity to consume — derived by requiring \(\partial h/\partial x > 0\) directly from the normalized capital law of motion \(h = (w+R_K x - \tilde{c})/(Be^\eta\tilde{c}^\gamma)\) via the quotient rule — is: \[\tilde{c}_x < \frac{\tilde{c}\,R_{K,t}}{\tilde{c} + \gamma(w_t + R_{K,t}x - \tilde{c})}\] at the competitive equilibrium. (This bound is tighter than the naive \(\tilde{c}_x < R_{K,t}\) because the denominator \(Be^\eta\tilde{c}^\gamma\) also increases in \(x\) through \(\tilde{c}\); the \(\gamma\) factor on the savings term \(w+R_K x-\tilde{c} > 0\) is the demographic dilution term absent from standard RCK normalizations. Assumption (A3) is standard in heterogeneous-agent RBC models; the monotone-policy property holds under analogous conditions in Stokey, Lucas, and Prescott (1989, Propositions 9.8–9.10) for the standard stochastic growth model and Aiyagari (1994, Lemma 1) for the precautionary-savings setting. A3 is maintained here as a standing assumption given the strict concavity of \(u(N)\) and the CRS technology.)

Proposition 3.1
In a complete-markets economy with idiosyncratic-shock insurance, the competitive equilibrium satisfies Labor-Power Dominance with respect to idiosyncratic risk. However, initial capital inequality violates Labor-Power Dominance with respect to the distribution of labor power. This violation exhibits greater persistence due to the demographic continuation term \((1-\gamma)\).

Proof (conditional on Assumption A3). Three steps.

Step (i) — Monotone capital-ratio ordering. Both dynasties share the aggregate state \(z_t = (\theta_t, \eta_t)\) and the competitive equilibrium policy function \(\tilde{c}(\cdot, z_t)\). Under competitive factor markets, dynasty \(i\) receives the common wage and rental income schedule induced by the representative firm, so the normalized capital law of motion is: \[x_{i,t+1} = h(x_{i,t}, z_t) \;\equiv\; \frac{w_t + R_{K,t}x_{i,t} \;-\; \tilde{c}(x_{i,t},z_t)}{Be^{\eta_t}\,\tilde{c}(x_{i,t},z_t)^\gamma}.\] Under (A3), differentiating \(h = N(x)/D(x)\) with \(N = w_t + R_{K,t}x - \tilde{c}\) and \(D = Be^{\eta_t}\tilde{c}^\gamma\) gives \(h_x = (R_{K,t}-\tilde{c}_x)/D - h\gamma\tilde{c}_x/\tilde{c}\); the A3 condition \(\tilde{c}_x < \tilde{c}R_{K,t}/[\tilde{c}+\gamma(w_t+R_{K,t}x-\tilde{c})]\) is precisely the condition that makes \(h_x > 0\). Consequently \(h(\cdot,z_t)\) is strictly increasing in \(x\) for all \(z_t\). By induction: \(x_{1,0} > x_{2,0} \Rightarrow x_{1,t} > x_{2,t}\) a.s. for all \(t \ge 0\).

Step (ii) — Strict reproductive-growth ordering. With idiosyncratic shocks \(\eta_{i,t}\) fully insured, dynasty \(i\)’s aggregate reproduction growth factor is \(g_{i,t} = Be^{\eta_t}\tilde{c}(x_{i,t},z_t)^\gamma\) (common aggregate shock \(\eta_t\), dynasty-specific \(\tilde{c}\)). Since \(\tilde{c}(x_{1,t},z_t) > \tilde{c}(x_{2,t},z_t)\) from Step (i) and \(\gamma > 0\): \(g_{1,t} > g_{2,t}\) a.s. for all \(t \ge 1\). With \(N_{1,0} = N_{2,0}\), the reproduction ratio \(N_{1,t}/N_{2,t} = \prod_{s=0}^{t-1}g_{1,s}/g_{2,s} > 1\) and is strictly increasing in \(t\).

Step (iii) — LPD violation via liminf. Compare allocation A (unequal: \(K_{1,0} > K_{2,0}\), common \(N_0\)) to allocation B (equal-endowment counterfactual: \(K_{i,0} = \bar{K}\), same \(N_0\)), where in the large-\(I\) atomistic closure \(\bar{K} \equiv \frac{1}{I}\sum_{i=1}^I K_{i,0}\) (equal per-dynasty share of aggregate initial capital; in the two-dynasty illustration this reduces to \((K_{1,0}+K_{2,0})/2\)), so aggregate initial capital is identical across the two allocations and \(\bar{x}_0 > x_{2,0}^A\). By Step (i), \(\bar{x}_t > x_{2,t}^A\) a.s. for all \(t \ge 0\); by Step (ii), \(\bar{g}_t > g_{2,t}^A\) a.s. for all \(t \ge 1\). The discounted cumulative \(N\)-difference for dynasty 2 is: \[S_T^2 \;=\; \sum_{t=0}^T \beta^t\bigl(N_{2,t}^A - N_{2,t}^B\bigr) \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^T \beta^t N_0 \Biggl(\prod_{s=0}^{t-1} g_{2,s}^A - \prod_{s=0}^{t-1} \bar{g}_s\Biggr).\] Each term in the sum is strictly negative (since \(\prod_{s=0}^{t-1} g_{2,s}^A < \prod_{s=0}^{t-1} \bar{g}_s\) by the strict growth-rate ordering from Step (ii)). Therefore \(S_T^2 < 0\) for all \(T \ge 1\); since each finite partial sum is strictly negative a.s., linearity of expectation also gives \(\mathbb{E}_0[S_T^2] < 0\) for every finite \(T \ge 1\). Hence \(\liminf_{T\to\infty} S_T^2 \le S_1^2 = \beta(g_{2,0}^A - \bar{g}_0)N_0 < 0\): allocation A does not LPD-dominate B for dynasty 2. The \((1-\gamma)\) amplification holds because the reproduction technology \(N_{i,t+1} = Be^{\eta_t}N_{i,t}^{1-\gamma}C_{i,t}^\gamma\) exhibits N-level persistence proportional to \((1-\gamma)\): higher \((1-\gamma)\) preserves existing \(N\)-ratio gaps between dynasties more strongly each period — through the \(N_{i,t}^{1-\gamma}\) elasticity — compounding the strictly negative N-level disadvantage multiplicatively across periods. \(\square\)

4. Internal Derivation of Interest Rates and Risk Premia

The materialist risk premium emerges from the stochastic discount factor \(m_{t+1} = \beta\lambda_{t+1}/\lambda_t\) once the second-order Schmitt-Grohé/Uribe perturbation described in Appendix A.2 is applied.6 A first-order log-linearization is insufficient: at first order the SDF carries zero conditional variance and all covariance risk premia vanish (see Appendix A.2 for the argument). As derived in Appendix A.2, the risk premium is stated as Theorem 4.1 below. Let lower-case variables denote log deviations.

Intuition. A stochastic discount factor is the pricing kernel that equates today’s asset price to tomorrow’s expected payoff adjusted for macroeconomic risk. In standard CCAPM, this kernel depends on aggregate consumption growth: assets that pay off poorly when consumption is low earn a risk premium. The materialist SDF derived from \(m_{t+1} = \beta\lambda_{t+1}/\lambda_t\) adds a second risk channel: because \(\lambda_t\) is the Bellman multiplier on reproductive capacity \(N_t\) — not on utility — the pricing kernel responds to demographic risk as well as consumption risk. An asset that pays off poorly precisely when society’s reproductive capacity contracts earns an additional risk premium beyond what CCAPM would assign. This is the \((1-\gamma)\Omega\) term.

The key structural feature is that \((1-\gamma)\Omega\) is not a free parameter. It is pinned by the cross-equation restriction \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\): the same CRS technology parameter \(\gamma\) that governs reproduction also constrains the effective risk price through Bellman curvature \(\psi_x\), the capital-ratio savings response \(\xi_\theta\), and the consumption response coefficient \(\varphi_\theta\). Standard CCAPM can match any observed risk premium by adjusting the risk-aversion parameter; the materialist framework cannot — which is what makes Theorem 4.1 a testable restriction rather than a calibration.

Theorem 4.1 (Conditional Materialist Risk Premium) Conditional on Closures (i)–(v) in Step (0) of Appendix A.2, and on:
(A1) the normalized value function \(\Psi(x)\) satisfies \(\Psi''(\bar{x}) < 0\) (strict concavity, standard SLP Ch.~4); and
(A2) \(\xi_\theta = (\bar{y} - \bar{c}\varphi_\theta)/(\bar{g}\bar{x}) - \gamma\varphi_\theta > 0\) (positive net capital-ratio savings response exceeding demographic dilution: \((\bar{y}-\bar{c}\varphi_\theta)/(\bar{g}\bar{x}) > \gamma\varphi_\theta\)).
Then the equilibrium risk premium on productive capital satisfies, to second order in the SG&U perturbation: for \(\sigma \ge 1\),

\[ \mathbb{E}_t[R_{K,t+1}] - R_{f,t} \approx \sigma \gamma \cdot \operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1}) + (1-\gamma) \cdot \Omega(\sigma, \gamma, \beta) \cdot \operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta n_{t+2}, R_{K,t+1}). \]

This statement is local: it is valid in the SG&U second-order neighborhood of the normalized stochastic steady state under Closures (i)-(v) in Appendix A.2, and is not claimed as a global exact representation away from that neighborhood.

(Here \(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1} \equiv \Delta\log(C_{t+1}/N_{t+1})\) is the log-growth of normalized per-capita consumption; \(\Delta n_{t+2} \equiv \log(N_{t+2}/N_{t+1})\) is the log-growth of labor power in period \(t+1\), capturing the period-\((t+1)\) reproductive shock through the SDF innovation; and \(R_{f,t}\) is the gross risk-free return. The \(\sigma\gamma\) loading on \(\Delta\tilde{c}\) is the second-order analogue of the standard CCAPM consumption-risk term; the \((1-\gamma)\Omega\) loading on \(\Delta n_{t+2}\) is the demographic continuation channel with no counterpart in any Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans formulation; the demographic terms in Barro and Becker (1989) dynastic-altruism models arise from parental preference over child utility rather than from a physical CRS reproduction technology, and are structurally distinct.)

The demographic-risk coefficient is given in closed form by the A.2 derivation: \[\Omega = -\frac{\psi_x\,\xi_\theta}{(1-\gamma)\,\gamma\,\varphi_\theta} > 0,\] where \(\psi_x \equiv \bar{x}\Psi''(\bar{x})/\Psi'(\bar{x}) < 0\) (A1), \(\xi_\theta \equiv (\bar{y}-\bar{c}\varphi_\theta)/(\bar{g}\bar{x}) - \gamma\varphi_\theta > 0\) (A2), and \(\varphi_\theta > 0\) (consumption-smoothing response to technology shock). The \(-\gamma\varphi_\theta\) term in \(\xi_\theta\) is the demographic dilution channel: higher \(\gamma\) (more consumption-dependent reproduction) directly suppresses the capital-ratio savings response to technology shocks by increasing denominator growth \(\hat{g}_t = \eta_t + \gamma\hat{\tilde{c}}_t\) — a structural damping effect absent from all standard RCK normalizations where growth is exogenous. The sign \(\Omega > 0\) follows directly: numerator \(-\psi_x\xi_\theta\) is negative times positive $= $ positive; denominator \((1-\gamma)\gamma\varphi_\theta > 0\). The parametric boundary \(\sigma \ge 1\) appears in the \(\psi_x\) curvature through the SS Bellman equations and ensures the marginal disutility of reproductive variance is non-negligible relative to the intertemporal substitution motive; it also operates through the net capital-ratio savings response \(\xi_\theta\) (A2): for \(\sigma < 1\) the reproductive objective’s curvature can reduce the investment residual \((\bar{y} - \bar{c}\varphi_\theta)\) and potentially violate A2. We impose \(\sigma \ge 1\) as a primitive parametric condition sufficient to guarantee A1 and A2 globally. The effective risk price \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\) is strictly decreasing in \(\gamma\) (taking the explicit \(\gamma\)-dependence of \(\xi_\theta\) into account, \(\partial[(1-\gamma)\Omega]/\partial\gamma = \psi_x/\gamma + \psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma^2\varphi_\theta) < 0\) under A1–A2), so the demographic channel’s contribution to the risk premium declines as reproduction becomes more consumption-dependent. Note: \(\Omega\) itself diverges as \(\gamma\to 1\) while \((1-\gamma)\to 0\); the product \((1-\gamma)\Omega\) converges to a finite positive constant \(-\psi_x\xi_\theta/\varphi_\theta > 0\), so the full premium does not vanish at \(\gamma=1\) instead the two channels merge as \(\Delta n_{t+2}\to\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}+\eta_{t+1}\) when \(\gamma=1\). More generally, under the maintained orthogonality of primitive shocks (\(\operatorname{Cov}_t(\eta_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1}) = 0\)), both covariance terms are proportional to the same scalar for any \(\gamma\in(0,1)\): \(\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta n_{t+2}, R_{K,t+1}) = \gamma\,\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1})\) (as shown in Appendix A.2). Substituting yields the equivalent single-factor form \(\gamma[\sigma + (1-\gamma)\Omega]\cdot\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1})\), where \(\gamma[\sigma+(1-\gamma)\Omega]\) is the total effective risk-aversion coefficient. The two-term presentation in Theorem 4.1 separates the consumption-risk interpretation from the structural materialist augmentation; the augmentation \((1-\gamma)^2\Omega > 0\) raises effective risk aversion above the standard \(\sigma\gamma\) level, establishing the materialist risk premium as a structural reinterpretation of the CCAPM coefficient rather than an independent additive factor.

5. Capitalist Institutions and Labor-Power Inequality

Each institutional arrangement alters the mechanism but not the fundamental result: under initial capital inequality, competitive factor markets cannot restore Labor-Power Dominance.

Autarky: Dynasties consume their own production. Labor-Power Dominance is violated due to unequal marginal products of capital.

Credit Markets: Equalize marginal products and raise total labor power, but the capital-rich dynasty receives interest income, widening absolute inequality in \(N\). Credit does not abolish hierarchy; it reorganizes it. In the monetary-yield hierarchy Huato (2026, p. 24) derives for the money form, all private ownership claims are priced as deviations from the yield on ultimate money, so that the capital-rich dynasty occupies the superior position in that hierarchy even where product-market efficiency rises. Following the logic of surplus-value formation Huato (2026, p. 89) traces from exploitation of living labor through to interest-bearing capital, the widening of dynasty-level claims under credit and labor markets is not a neutral financial redistribution but — in the present model’s terms — a differentiated command over the future reproduction of commodified labor power.7

Labor Markets: Equalize wages and per-capita consumption in steady state, but the capital-rich dynasty retains a transitional advantage. The demographic continuation term amplifies permanent divergence in dynasty-level labor-power trajectories.

Proposition 5.1
Private ownership satisfies Labor-Power Dominance with respect to idiosyncratic risk but violates it with respect to initial capital inequality. The violation grows with \((1-\gamma)\).

Proof (conditional on Assumption A3). The LPD violation under private ownership parallels Proposition 3.1 Steps (i)–(iii), with \(\tilde{c}(\cdot)\) now the competitive equilibrium policy under private ownership and with the same maintained complete-markets insurance against idiosyncratic shocks as in the dynasty benchmark above. The monotonicity argument from Proposition 3.1 Step (i) carries over under the competitive factor-income law of motion \(h(\cdot,z_t)\), so the \(x\)-gap remains preserved for all \(t\); because the idiosyncratic shocks remain fully insured in this proposition’s benchmark, the common-shock growth ordering \(g_{1,t} > g_{2,t}\) then follows exactly as in Proposition 3.1 Step (ii). Step (iii) therefore carries over to yield \(\liminf_T S_T^2 < 0\).

The additional gap that Proposition 3.1’s proof does not exclude — that competitive equilibrium dynamics might generate stochastic convergence in \(x_{i,t}\) across dynasties, potentially erasing the \(N\)-gap — does not block the LPD violation. Even if \(x_{1,t} - x_{2,t} \to 0\) asymptotically, the \(N\)-ratio \(N_{1,t}/N_{2,t} = \prod_{s=0}^{t-1}g_{1,s}/g_{2,s}\) accumulates all prior growth-rate differentials multiplicatively. Any finite window \([0,t^*]\) with \(g_{1,s} > g_{2,s}\) locks in \(N_{1,t}/N_{2,t} > 1\) for all \(t > t^*\), since subsequent equal growth rates preserve but cannot erase the accumulated ratio. The discounted \(N\)-difference \(S_T^2\) therefore remains strictly negative regardless of asymptotic \(x\)-convergence, and \(\liminf_T S_T^2 < 0\) holds. \(\square\)

6. Incomplete Markets, Precautionary Saving, and Fictitious Capital

Under incomplete markets, dynasties face uninsurable idiosyncratic shocks to physical reproduction and must self-insure.

Assumption 6.A (Jensen Dominance): The direct concavity penalty on \(\mathbb{E}[N_{t+1}]\) from uninsurable idiosyncratic variance strictly dominates the general-equilibrium increase in aggregate capital accumulation induced by precautionary saving. (Assumption 6.A is a primitive of this analysis. Its parametric boundary — the \((\gamma,\sigma)\)-region in which the Jensen concavity penalty strictly dominates the GE precautionary-savings offset — constitutes the central quantitative question for the empirical companion paper, which will derive that boundary from model parameters and test it against cohort fertility and asset-pricing data. Note: this dominance condition is not monotone in \(\sigma\). Higher \(\sigma\) strengthens both the Jensen penalty and the GE precautionary-savings offset — the latter because higher risk aversion raises the precautionary demand for capital accumulation, as in Aiyagari (1994). The \((\gamma,\sigma)\)-boundary at which Jensen dominance holds therefore depends on the relative magnitudes of these two opposing effects. The \((1-\gamma)\) demographic dilution term dampens the capital-ratio savings response to idiosyncratic shocks (as shown in Appendix A.2 Step (2)), making the GE offset weaker than in a standard RCK model for any given \(\sigma\); this structural damping supports the dominance condition at moderate idiosyncratic variance, but it does not constitute a monotone implication from \(\sigma\) alone.)

Proposition 6.1 (conditional on Assumptions 6.A and A3)
Under Assumption 6.A, incomplete markets violate Labor-Power Dominance with respect to idiosyncratic risk. Total expected labor power is strictly lower than under the complete-markets benchmark. The demographic continuation term amplifies this inefficiency through non-linear compounding of reproductive deficits. (Proof sketch: Without state-contingent claims, dynasties face uninsurable idiosyncratic variance. For admissible internal calibrations \(0 < \gamma < 1\), the reproduction function \(N_{t+1} = B e^{\eta_t} N_t^{1-\gamma} C_t^{\gamma}\) is strictly concave with respect to \(N_t\). With mean-zero shocks independent of aggregate variables, Jensen’s Inequality applied to the cross-dynasty distribution of \(N_{i,t}\) (which carries strictly positive variance under incomplete markets due to uninsured \(\eta_{i,t}\) shocks) gives \(\frac{1}{I}\sum_i N_{i,t+1} < f\!\left(\frac{1}{I}\sum_i N_{i,t}\right)\), using strict concavity of \(N_{t+1}\) in \(N_t\) with each dynasty’s \(C_{i,t}\) held at its individually optimal path. Under Assumption 6.A, this partial-equilibrium Jensen penalty is not offset by GE precautionary capital accumulation, and the LPD violation holds. (Note: the Jensen argument establishes a one-period reduction in \(\mathbb{E}[N_{t+1}]\); the infinite-horizon LPD conclusion — that the liminf of the discounted cumulative \(N\)-difference is strictly positive — now rests on the proof argument of Propositions 3.1 and 5.1. Under Assumption (A3), the monotone-mapping and liminf steps extend to the aggregate case, replacing the inter-dynasty comparison with the incomplete-markets vs. complete-markets benchmark comparison. The Jensen step establishes the one-period reduction; the infinite-horizon LPD claim holds conditionally under Assumptions (A3) and 6.A.)

Fictitious Capital

The derivation below supplies the formal risk-pricing foundation for fictitious capital within the Bellman apparatus — the specific gap that Harvey (1982) and Foley (1982) identified but could not close without a dynamic stochastic model anchored in the conditions of labor-power reproduction. The institutional dimensions (circuit-of-capital closure, class-distributional consequences of the yield hierarchy, endogenous credit-money creation) constitute the terrain for future companion work; they extend and concretize the present formal result rather than replace it.8

(Note on terminology: the paper uses “fictitious capital” in Huato’s (2026, pp. 101–104) yield-hierarchy sense — an asset whose price capitalizes a ranked structure of claims on future social labor through the material conditions of reproduction — which is broader than Marx’s strict Vol. III definition of fictitious capital as the capitalization of surplus-value streams produced in the sphere of production. The two usages share the capitalization-of-future-income structure but differ on what is being capitalized: the paper’s mechanism is precautionary demand for insurance against reproductive-risk variance, not a claim on surplus value per se. The distributional consequences linking the bond to class reproduction require the heterogeneous-agent portfolio closure deferred to future work.)
The safe bond (“money of last resort”) is priced by the corrected SDF via \(\mathbb{E}_t[m_{t+1} R_{f,t}] = 1\).9 In Huato’s formulation this safe return is the apex of a monetary hierarchy that is simultaneously a yield hierarchy: the bond approaches the yield on ultimate money \(r_0\), while every subordinate ownership claim deviates from that anchor in proportion to its specific risk premium (Huato 2026, pp. 24, 57–58). In the present model \(R_f = 1/\mathbb{E}_t[m_{t+1}]\) is determined endogenously by demographic risk \(\sigma_\eta^2\) and the planner’s curvature parameters \((\sigma,\gamma)\); this is structurally distinct from Huato’s \(r_0\), which is anchored in the monetary hierarchy’s historical conditions and insensitive to reproductive variance. The two rates respond differently to reproductive variance: a rise in \(\sigma_\eta^2\) lowers \(R_f\) — precautionary demand for the safe asset drives up bond prices and compresses the risk-free yield — while \(r_0\) is insensitive to demographic variance.10 The identification \(R_f \approx r_0\) holds approximately when demographic variance is small and the monetary hierarchy’s historically anchored \(r_0\) converges to the certainty-equivalent reproduction rate; the general circuit-of-capital closure that formally establishes this convergence is deferred to future work. That pricing relation clarifies the sense in which capitalist wealth appears as fictitious capital (Huato 2026, pp. 101–104): what is capitalized here is not an autonomous financial substance but a ranked structure of claims on future social labor, discounted through the material conditions of labor-power reproduction. The Bellman structure used here is a formal representation of that social reproduction constraint — not a philosophical substitute for value-form theory, but a dynamic state-space that makes the Harvey/Foley claim that finance is discipline over future labor power mathematically tractable. Because \(m_{t+1}\) incorporates the demographic continuation term, uncertainty about future labor power raises the price of safety and depresses investment in real capital. The demographic SDF thus converts reproductive uncertainty into a systematic demand for the safe asset: capitalist finance appears here not as an autonomous monetary sphere but as the institutional form through which the risk of future labor-power deficits is priced and redistributed across dynasties. By capitalizing future income streams at the demographic SDF, the safe bond aggregates the cost of missing insurance markets. However, translating this aggregate-level crowding out into the specific reproduction of class inequality requires a fully specified heterogeneous-agent portfolio closure, which is deferred to future work.

7. Conclusion

By restoring constant returns to scale to the stochastic reproduction function, replacing the subjective utility metric with the objective physical index \(N\), and deriving asset prices as conditional closed-form outputs from the Euler equation, this paper establishes four structural results. First, the materialist Bellman problem produces a stochastic discount factor with a demographic continuation term \((1-\gamma)\) absent from standard Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans formulations (and structurally distinct from the dynastic-altruism terms in Barro and Becker (1989), which arise from parental preference over child utility rather than from a physical CRS reproduction technology); this term is not a calibration device but a consequence of CRS reproductive technology. Second, Theorem 4.1 is established in Appendix A.2 as a conditional second-order SG&U risk-premium formula: \(E_t[R_{K,t+1}] - R_{f,t} \approx \sigma\gamma\cdot\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1}) + (1-\gamma)\Omega\cdot\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta n_{t+2}, R_{K,t+1})\), with structural risk-premium coefficient \(\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/[(1-\gamma)\gamma\varphi_\theta]\) whose sign is proved positive under A1 and A2. Crucially, the equivalent single-factor representation \(\gamma[\sigma+(1-\gamma)\Omega]\cdot\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1})\) makes visible what distinguishes this framework from standard CCAPM: the effective risk-aversion coefficient \(\gamma[\sigma+(1-\gamma)\Omega]\) is not freely chosen but structurally constrained by reproduction technology parameters \((\gamma, \psi_x, \xi_\theta, \varphi_\theta)\) through the cross-equation restriction \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\) — a testable constraint that standard CCAPM cannot generate. Third, Labor-Power Dominance supplies the welfare criterion under which competitive institutions generate structurally necessary violations when initial capital is unequally distributed (Proposition 3.1), when those gaps persist under a monotone policy function (Proposition 5.1), and when incomplete insurance markets amplify the Jensen penalty on aggregate reproductive capacity (Proposition 6.1). Fourth, the safe bond’s risk premium and the crowding-out of real investment are grounded in the same demographic SDF that prices the risky asset, closing the fictitious-capital argument structurally rather than interpretatively — as distinct from the conceptual treatments in Harvey (2010) and Durand (2017).

The primary empirical frontier opened by this framework is the structural identification of the effective risk price \((1-\gamma)\Omega\), from which the demographic coefficient \(\Omega\) can be extracted. The materialist SDF uses the normalized consumption measure \(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1} = \Delta(C/N)_{t+1}\) — consumption per unit of labor power — rather than standard aggregate consumption per capita; these differ when demographic composition shifts non-uniformly (e.g., falling fertility accompanied by rising old-age dependency), providing a second observational wedge against standard CCAPM. The cross-equation restriction \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\) connects the effective risk-aversion coefficient in the SDF to three observables: the reproduction elasticity \(\gamma\) (identifiable from cohort fertility data), the capital-ratio savings response \(\xi_\theta\) (recoverable from investment-share dynamics under the model’s state-space closure), and the technology-shock response coefficient \(\varphi_\theta\) (recoverable from the response of normalized consumption to identified productivity shocks). The remaining Bellman-curvature term \(\psi_x \equiv \bar{x}\Psi''(\bar{x})/\Psi'(\bar{x})\) is mathematically identifiable via perturbation methods, though not empirically provided here. Testing this restriction against standard CCAPM — and deriving the GMM moment conditions for \(\Omega\) from joint demographic and asset pricing data — is the subject of a companion paper.

This paper establishes a materialist framework placing the reproduction of labor power at the center of long-run growth and finance. Its formal strategy is one of deliberate methodological immanence: the same Bellman apparatus that standard macro deploys to establish the efficiency of competitive institutions is here turned to demonstrate that those institutions generate structurally necessary violations of the materialist welfare criterion — competitive factor markets do not fail in this model by failing to solve their own Paretian optimization problem but by solving it perfectly, thereby compounding the reproductive deficits that LPD formalizes. Throughout, the paper operates within the stochastic recursive grammar of dynamic programming — a deliberate methodological choice whose condition of possibility is stationary, ergodic structure — a constraint the paper accepts because it is precisely the condition for deriving the critique from within the apparatus that establishes competitive efficiency. The stationarity assumption is the price of admission for immanent proof, not a concession to neoclassical convention. The stationary pricing kernel of Theorem 4.1 is the formal result that any future extension incorporating crisis dynamics, qualitative class transitions, or non-ergodic reproductive trajectories must be consistent with — not as a claim that capitalism’s history is ergodic. The immediate extensions are: (i) the empirical companion paper, identifying \(\Omega\) and testing the cross-equation restriction using reproduction-technology moments; (ii) a class-specific Bellman extension — separating liquidity-constrained working dynasties from accumulating capitalist dynasties — that would supply the heterogeneous-agent general equilibrium with portfolio choice needed to derive the full class-distributional consequences of the LPD violation in a complete factor-market closure; and (iii) the axiomatics of LPD relative to Paretian and utilitarian orderings, sketched in §3 and deferred to future work.

Appendix: Key Mathematical Derivations

A.1 Full Corrected Euler Equation

The structural Euler equation is obtained by combining the first-order condition with the iterated \(V_N\) envelope. The first-order condition gives: \[\lambda_t = \beta\mathbb{E}_t\Bigl[V_N(N_{t+1},K_{t+1})\cdot\gamma\frac{N_{t+1}}{C_t}\Bigr].\] Substituting the \(V_N\) envelope one period forward: \[V_N(N_{t+1},K_{t+1}) = u'(N_{t+1}) + \lambda_{t+1}w_{t+1} + \beta\mathbb{E}_{t+1}\Bigl[V_N(N_{t+2},K_{t+2})\cdot(1-\gamma)\frac{N_{t+2}}{N_{t+1}}\Bigr]\] yields the open-form structural Euler equation stated in 2. The key departure from Ramsey-Cass-Koopmans is the demographic continuation term \((1-\gamma)N_{t+1}/N_t\): setting \(\gamma=1\) collapses the reproduction technology to a pure consumption-to-population link, eliminating the continuation term and recovering a standard Euler equation. The closed-form reduction of this open-form condition is established by the normalization and perturbation in Appendix A.2.

A.2 Materialist Risk Premium — Full Derivation

Because the production and reproduction technologies are constant returns to scale in \((N_t, K_t, C_t)\) jointly, the value function satisfies the homogeneity condition \(V(xN, xK) = x^{1-\sigma}V(N,K)\), implying the correct functional form is: \[V(N_t, K_t) = N_t^{1-\sigma}\,\Psi\!\left(\frac{K_t}{N_t}\right)\] where \(\Psi\) is a function of the capital-labor ratio alone. Existence and uniqueness of the value function satisfying this homogeneous form follow from the standard contraction-mapping argument for bounded-growth Bellman operators (Stokey, Lucas, and Prescott 1989, ch. 4); the contraction condition stated in closure (i) of step (0) is the relevant sufficient condition here. The log-linear value-function ansatz \(v(n_t,k_t) = A_N n_t + A_K k_t\) (log-deviations of raw states) is consistent with this homogeneous form only in the knife-edge \(\sigma=1\) case; for general \(\sigma \neq 1\) it is incompatible with the model’s Bellman structure and must not be used.

The derivation proceeds in five steps: (0) Maintained Assumptions: (i) To ensure the normalized policy function exists as a contraction mapping, we impose the strict contraction bound \(\beta \mathbb{E}[\left(N_{t+1}/N_t\right)^{1-\sigma}] < 1\) (note: this is an implicit condition since \(N_{t+1}/N_t = Be^{\eta}\tilde{c}_t^\gamma\) involves the policy function \(\tilde{c}\); a primitive sufficient condition on exogenous parameters requires bounding \(\mathbb{E}[e^{(1-\sigma)\eta}]\) by \(1/\beta B^{1-\sigma}\) at the planner’s steady-state consumption share, which pins the condition on \(\sigma\), \(\gamma\), \(\beta\), \(B\), and the shock variance); (ii) the normalized stochastic steady state \((\bar{x},\bar{\tilde{c}})\) is assumed unique — to be verified by confirming the linearized Bellman-operator Jacobian has all eigenvalues strictly inside the unit circle at the fixed point; (iii) the BGP growth factor \(g_N \equiv \mathbb{E}[N_{t+1}/N_t]\) is assumed finite and positive, ensuring stationarity of the normalized system after \(N_t\) is factored out. The sign conditions \(\Omega > 0\) and \(\partial\Omega/\partial\gamma < 0\) are proved conditional on all five closures and Assumptions (A1)–(A2); see Theorem 4.1 and Step (5) below. Two further closures govern the perturbation step specifically: (iv) while the contraction bound (closure i) ensures existence of the value function, local uniqueness of the normalized SS (closure ii) formally requires saddle-path stability of the resulting policy function; (v) the SG&U second-order perturbation is locally valid in a neighborhood of the SS; the maintained log-normal shock distribution bounds the probability of large realizations escaping this neighborhood, but does not guarantee global accuracy. Sign conditions are proved conditional on all five closures and Assumptions (A1)–(A2). We further assume the state and shock variables admit log-normal innovations with finite second moments. The second-order perturbation is performed directly on the fully stationary stochastically normalized variables \((x_t, \tilde{c}_t)\), with the unit-root variable \(N_t\) factored entirely out. The sign conditions \(\Omega > 0\) and \(\partial\Omega/\partial\gamma < 0\) are proved for this stochastically normalized system conditional on Assumptions (A1)–(A2); (1) Normalized Bellman. Define \(x_t \equiv K_t/N_t\), \(\tilde{c}_t \equiv C_t/N_t\), \(g_t \equiv N_{t+1}/N_t = Be^{\eta_t}\tilde{c}_t^\gamma\), and \(y_t \equiv Y_t/N_t = Ae^{\theta_t}x_t^\alpha\). The normalized capital law of motion is \(x_{t+1} = (y_t - \tilde{c}_t + (1-\delta)x_t)/g_t\). Substituting \(V(N,K) = N^{1-\sigma}\Psi(x)\) into the Bellman equation and dividing by \(N_t^{1-\sigma}\) yields the normalized fixed-point problem: \[\Psi(x_t) = \max_{\tilde{c}_t}\!\left\{\frac{1}{1-\sigma} + \beta\mathbb{E}_t\!\left[g_t^{1-\sigma}\Psi(x_{t+1})\right]\right\}.\] The first-order condition for \(\tilde{c}_t\), using \(\partial x_{t+1}/\partial\tilde{c}_t = -g_t^{-1} - \gamma x_{t+1}/\tilde{c}_t\), is: \[\beta\mathbb{E}_t\!\left[g_t^{1-\sigma}\!\left(\frac{\gamma(1-\sigma)\Psi(x_{t+1})}{\tilde{c}_t} - \Psi'(x_{t+1})\!\left(\frac{1}{g_t}+\frac{\gamma x_{t+1}}{\tilde{c}_t}\right)\!\right)\right] = 0.\] (2) Stochastic steady state and linearized policy function. At the deterministic steady state (\(\theta=\eta=0\), \(x_t=\bar{x}\), \(\tilde{c}_t=\bar{c}\)), define \(\Phi(x) \equiv (1-\sigma)\Psi(x) - x\Psi'(x)\). The three SS conditions are: \[\bar{g} = B\bar{c}^\gamma, \qquad \bar{g}\bar{x} = A\bar{x}^\alpha + (1-\delta)\bar{x} - \bar{c}, \qquad \bar{g}\gamma\Phi(\bar{x}) = \bar{c}\Psi'(\bar{x}),\] together with the capital Euler \(\beta\bar{g}^{1-\sigma}\bar{R}_K = 1\) where \(\bar{R}_K = \alpha A\bar{x}^{\alpha-1} + 1-\delta\). The linearized policy function around SS takes the form \[\hat{\tilde{c}}_t = \varphi_x\hat{x}_t + \varphi_\theta\theta_t + \varphi_\eta\eta_t, \quad \hat{x}_{t+1} = \xi_x\hat{x}_t + \xi_\theta\theta_t + \xi_\eta\eta_t,\] where \(\hat{a} \equiv \log(a_t/\bar{a})\) and the coefficients \((\varphi_x,\varphi_\theta,\varphi_\eta,\xi_x,\xi_\theta,\xi_\eta)\) are determined by log-linearizing the FOC and normalized Bellman envelope around the SS. The key coefficient — derived by log-linearizing \(x_{t+1} = (y_t - \tilde{c}_t + (1-\delta)x_t)/g_t\) fully via the quotient rule, accounting for \(g_t = Be^{\eta_t}\tilde{c}_t^\gamma\)’s dependence on \(\tilde{c}_t\) in the denominator — is: \[\xi_\theta = \frac{\bar{y} - \bar{c}\varphi_\theta}{\bar{g}\bar{x}} - \gamma\varphi_\theta,\] which governs the net capital-ratio response to a technology shock. The \(-\gamma\varphi_\theta\) term is the demographic dilution correction: a positive technology shock raises \(\tilde{c}_t\) (via \(\varphi_\theta > 0\)), which in turn raises \(g_t = Be^{\eta_t}\tilde{c}_t^\gamma\) (the denominator), partially offsetting the numerator’s increase. Standard RCK normalizations omit this correction because their growth rate is exogenous and \(g_t\) does not depend on \(\tilde{c}_t\). (3) Shadow price in log-deviations. From \(V_K(N_t,K_t) = N_t^{-\sigma}\Psi'(x_t)\) and the definition \(\lambda_t \equiv \beta\mathbb{E}_t[V_K(N_{t+1},K_{t+1})] = \beta N_{t+1}^{-\sigma}\Psi'(x_{t+1})\) (the expectation collapses exactly since \(N_{t+1} = Be^{\eta_t}N_t^{1-\gamma}C_t^\gamma\) and \(x_{t+1} = K_{t+1}/N_{t+1}\) are both strictly \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable, with all shocks \((\eta_t,\theta_t)\) realized before the period-\(t\) choice \(C_t\) is made): \[\hat{\lambda}_t = -\sigma\hat{n}_{t+1} + \psi_x\hat{x}_{t+1},\] where \(\psi_x \equiv \bar{x}\Psi''(\bar{x})/\Psi'(\bar{x})\) is the curvature elasticity of \(\Psi'\) at the steady state. This expression implicitly incorporates the recursive \((1-\gamma)\) demographic continuation through \(\Psi'\), since \(\Psi\) is the Bellman fixed point that has absorbed the full infinite-horizon reproduction structure. (4) Second-order perturbation and SDF innovation. From \(m_{t+1} = \beta\lambda_{t+1}/\lambda_t\): \[\log m_{t+1} \approx \log\beta - \sigma(\hat{n}_{t+2} - \hat{n}_{t+1}) + \psi_x(\hat{x}_{t+2} - \hat{x}_{t+1}).\] The innovation to \(\log m_{t+1}\) driven by period-\((t+1)\) shocks \((\eta_{t+1},\theta_{t+1})\) is: \[\widehat{m}_{t+1} \equiv \log m_{t+1} - \mathbb{E}_t[\log m_{t+1}] = -\sigma\hat{n}_{t+2}^{\text{innov}} + \psi_x\hat{x}_{t+2}^{\text{innov}}\] with \(\hat{n}_{t+2}^{\text{innov}} = (1+\gamma\varphi_\eta)\eta_{t+1} + \gamma\varphi_\theta\theta_{t+1}\) and \(\hat{x}_{t+2}^{\text{innov}} = \xi_\eta\eta_{t+1} + \xi_\theta\theta_{t+1}\). Decomposing \(\hat{n}_{t+2}^{\text{innov}} = \eta_{t+1} + \gamma\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}^{\text{innov}}\) makes explicit that \(\widehat{m}_{t+1} = -\sigma\gamma\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}^{\text{innov}} - \sigma\eta_{t+1} + \psi_x\hat{x}_{t+2}^{\text{innov}}\). At first order in the Schmitt-Groh’{e}–Uribe sense (linear policy function with \(\Psi^{(1)'}\) constant), \(\psi_x = 0\) and \(\hat{m}_{t+1}\) carries zero conditional variance with respect to \(r_{K,t+1}\) (since \(R_{K,t+1} = \alpha Ae^{\theta_{t+1}}x_{t+1}^{\alpha-1}+1-\delta\) and \(x_{t+1}\) is \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable). The \(\psi_x\) term requires \(\Psi''(\bar{x})\neq 0\) — equivalently, a second-order Taylor expansion of \(\Psi\) around the SS — and this is the non-degenerate channel that generates a non-zero risk premium. (5) Risk premium and the materialist coefficient \(\Omega\). Because \(R_{K,t+1}\) depends on period-\((t+1)\) shocks only through \(\theta_{t+1}\) (with \(x_{t+1}\) predetermined at \(t\)), the stochastic component is \(r_{K,t+1}^{\text{innov}} = \rho_\theta\theta_{t+1}\) where \(\rho_\theta \equiv \alpha A\bar{x}^{\alpha-1}/\bar{R}_K\). Applying \(\mathbb{E}_t[R_{K,t+1}] - R_{f,t} \approx -\operatorname{Cov}_t(\log m_{t+1}, r_{K,t+1})\): \[\mathbb{E}_t[R_{K,t+1}] - R_{f,t} = \sigma\gamma\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1}) + \left(-\frac{\psi_x\xi_\theta}{\gamma\varphi_\theta}\right)\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta n_{t+2}, R_{K,t+1}),\] using \(\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1},R_{K,t+1}) = \varphi_\theta\rho_\theta\sigma_\theta^2\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta n_{t+2},R_{K,t+1}) = \gamma\varphi_\theta\rho_\theta\sigma_\theta^2\) (where \(\operatorname{Cov}_t(\eta_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1}) = 0\) because \(R_{K,t+1}\) depends only on \(\theta_{t+1}\) and the predetermined \(x_{t+1}\), and the primitive shocks \(\theta_{t+1}\perp\eta_{t+1}\) are maintained orthogonal throughout — a standard assumption stated in Step (0)). Matching with the Theorem 4.1 formula \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\) identifies:

If aggregate productivity and reproductive shocks are correlated in observed data, this baseline covariance mapping is contaminated and empirical identification of \(\Omega\) must jointly estimate that correlation structure. \[\Omega = -\frac{\psi_x\,\xi_\theta}{(1-\gamma)\,\gamma\,\varphi_\theta}.\] Sign of \(\Omega\). Under Assumptions (A1)–(A2) stated in Theorem 4.1: (A1) implies \(\psi_x < 0\); (A2) implies \(\xi_\theta > 0\); \(\varphi_\theta > 0\) because a positive technology shock raises permanent income and consumption responds positively at the SS linearization — formally, log-linearizing the Euler equation at SS gives \(\hat{\tilde{c}}_t = \varphi_x\hat{x}_t + \varphi_\theta\theta_t\) with \(\varphi_\theta > 0\) (see Stokey, Lucas, and Prescott 1989, chs.~4–5 for the policy-function sign in the standard stochastic growth model); \(\gamma(1-\gamma)>0\). Hence \(\Omega = -(\text{neg})(\text{pos})/((\text{pos})^3) > 0\). Declining demographic risk price. \((1-\gamma)\Omega = -\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma\varphi_\theta)\) is strictly decreasing in \(\gamma\) (partial derivative \(\psi_x\xi_\theta/(\gamma^2\varphi_\theta) < 0\)), so the demographic channel’s contribution to the effective risk price falls as \(\gamma\) rises. At \(\gamma=1\) the factorization through \((1-\gamma)\) and \(\Omega\) separately becomes singular, but the product converges to the finite positive constant \(-\psi_x\xi_\theta/\varphi_\theta > 0\); the full demographic premium does not vanish at this boundary.

A.3 Labor-Power Dominance (Formal) Allocation \(A\) Labor-Power Dominates allocation \(B\) under a discounted variant of the Ramsey-Weizsäcker overtaking criterion — replacing undiscounted partial sums with \(\beta^t\)-weighted partial sums — if (i) for every dynasty \(i\), \(\liminf_{T \to \infty} \mathbb{E}_0\left[\sum_{t=0}^T \beta^t (N_{i,t}^A - N_{i,t}^B)\right] \ge 0\), and (ii) for at least one dynasty the inequality is strictly positive. No spectral radius restriction on \(\beta g_N\) is imposed as part of the LPD definition; the criterion is adopted precisely to handle the case where \(N_t\) grows along the equilibrium path. The classical Ramsey-Weizsäcker criterion uses undiscounted partial sums; the \(\beta^t\)-weighting preserves the overtaking structure while maintaining commensurability with the dynastic Bellman objective. The Bellman transversality condition \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\beta^T \mathbb{E}[V_K K_T] = 0\) is a separate optimality requirement.
