# A Progressing Problemshift: A Lakatosian Rational Reconstruction of the Sustenance-Capacity-as-Numéraire Research Programme
Carlos Galindo Escajeda
June 8, 2026

## Abstract

The heterodox research programme reconstructed here comprises five
papers. Four of them — a theory of value, a structural derivation of
rent, an empirical study of a housing subsidy, and a policy defence of
rent regulation — together form a four-ply protective belt around one
hard core (sustenance capacity as the numéraire of value, and a
Sustenance-Capacity Dominance criterion that replaces rather than
refines Pareto efficiency), worked by one positive heuristic (immanent
critique using orthodoxy’s own apparatus). This paper is the fifth: the
rational reconstruction itself, and it asks a deliberately bounded
question — not whether the programme is *true*, but whether its
development is *progressive* in Lakatos’s sense. The answer is split, by
necessity. The programme is **theoretically progressive**: a documented
belt revision converted an unproven demographic-risk conjecture into a
signed, closed-form restriction that *reduces* the modeller’s degrees of
freedom, the structural opposite of an ad hoc rescue. It is
**non-degenerating**: under recorded attacks on the core itself, the
core was preserved by belt revision and principled refusal, never by
inventing untestable auxiliaries. But it is **empirically progressing,
not yet empirically progressive**: the one corroborated prediction is,
by the empirical paper’s own admission, observationally equivalent
between the programme and its orthodox rival — shared content, not the
corroborated *novel* fact Lakatos requires — while the programme’s
discriminating predictions remain a declared, falsifiable frontier. The
reconstruction is unusual in being checkable against a contemporaneous
construction record, and in demonstrating the no-overclaim discipline by
demoting its own strongest-looking result. We claim only this: the
programme is now in a form where its merits can be appraised, rather
than dismissed a priori — and that, against the usual fate of heterodox
work, is the contribution.

## 1. Introduction — a programme, and the question of appraisal

Heterodox research in economics is routinely refused appraisal rather
than refuted. Its premises are declared inadmissible at the threshold —
too political, too unorthodox in their primitives, insufficiently
neutral — and the question of whether a given body of heterodox work is
*internally progressive or degenerating* is never reached.[^1] This
paper takes one recent body of such work and insists on reaching that
question.

The object of appraisal is the four-paper belt. A theory paper takes the
sustenance of the working population, rather than subjective utility, as
the primitive in which value is reckoned, and replaces Pareto efficiency
with a criterion it calls Sustenance-Capacity Dominance. A structural
paper derives, from that criterion, a sustenance-consistent ceiling on
rent. An empirical paper studies the capitalisation of a housing subsidy
into rents. A policy paper argues that the orthodox objection to rent
regulation is inapplicable to the financialised market that actually
exists. The four are not four arguments; they are one argument
distributed across four artifacts. This reconstruction is itself the
fifth paper in the programme — the meta-level appraisal that stands
outside the belt it examines.

The appraisal question worth asking is not “is the programme true?”
Lakatos’s central methodological move was to separate the *truth* of a
research programme from its *progressivity* — whether, over time, it
generates excess content and meets criticism by adding to its
explanatory reach rather than by patching anomalies \[Lakatos 1970\]. A
programme’s hard core, on this account, is held immune *by the
methodological decision of its practitioners*; the core is not what gets
tested. What gets tested is the protective belt, and the appraisal
verdict turns on *how the belt is revised under pressure*. This paper
addresses only that verdict. It is the central discipline of what
follows that we do not convert the appraisal into an endorsement of the
core: showing that a programme develops progressively is not showing
that its founding commitment is correct, and the two must not be allowed
to bleed into one another.

The contribution is threefold and modest. First, a rational
reconstruction of a heterodox economics programme in explicit Lakatosian
terms — uncommon in itself. Second, a reconstruction that is
*checkable*: the programme was developed under a recorded sequence of
adversarial review, so the claim “the belt was revised progressively
while the core stayed fixed” can be audited against a contemporaneous
construction record rather than rested on retrospective narrative.
Third, and most important methodologically, a reconstruction that
performs the discipline it describes — including by demoting the
programme’s own most rhetorically convenient result when that result
does not, in fact, discriminate the programme from its rival.

Section 2 sets out the Lakatosian apparatus and justifies its selection.
Sections 3–5 identify the hard core, the four-ply belt, and the positive
heuristic. Section 6 delivers the split appraisal. Section 7 treats the
construction record as bounded corroboration. Section 8 states the
limits, including the conflict of interest inherent in reconstructing
one’s own programme. Section 9 concludes.

## 2. The Lakatosian apparatus (imported, not defended)

Lakatos’s methodology of scientific research programmes distinguishes a
*hard core* of central commitments, held immune from refutation by the
practitioners’ methodological decision; a *protective belt* of auxiliary
hypotheses that bear the brunt of testing and are revised when anomalies
arise; and a *positive heuristic* that directs how the belt is to be
developed \[Lakatos 1970\]. The unit of appraisal is the programme over
time, not the individual paper or the isolated test.

The crucial feature for what follows is that the core is held immune *by
decision*. This is a description of how programmes actually allocate the
risk of refutation — anomalies are charged to the belt, and the core is
protected so long as the practitioners choose to protect it. That choice
is itself revisable: practitioners of a degenerating programme do,
eventually, abandon their cores. Immunity, then, is a methodological
stance, not a dogma, and a programme earns no credit merely for holding
a core fixed. It earns credit only if the core is held fixed *while the
belt does progressive work* — and, as Section 6.2 insists, only if the
core was genuinely *attacked* and nonetheless preserved without ad hoc
rescue.

Lakatos’s appraisal criterion is comparative and historical. A
problemshift is *theoretically progressive* if each modification of the
belt predicts some novel fact the predecessor did not; it is
*empirically progressive* if some of that excess content is
corroborated; and it is *degenerating* if the belt is revised only to
accommodate known anomalies, generating no new testable content
\[Lakatos 1970\]. These are graded, not binary, and they are judged over
a development sequence — which is precisely what a documented
construction history supplies.

Why Lakatos here, and not Kuhn or Laudan? Kuhn’s apparatus of paradigms
and normal science lacks a fine-grained, within-programme criterion for
distinguishing progress from degeneration; it describes the sociology of
theory change better than it adjudicates it \[Kuhn 1962\]. Laudan’s
problem-solving model offers an appraisal criterion, but one keyed to a
programme’s net problem-solving effectiveness across empirical and
conceptual problems — harder to operationalise from a textual record,
and less naturally mapped onto a programme with a single declared
normative primitive \[Laudan 1977\]. Lakatos’s core/belt architecture
maps directly onto a multi-paper programme built around one ontological
commitment with a logged development history. The choice is a scoping
decision, defended as such, and Section 8 records the meta-objection
that the framework itself may be ill-suited to economics.

## 3. The hard core — identified, and (by design) not defended

The programme has a single, stable hard core, and it is stated as such
in the theory paper: value is reckoned in the sustenance of the working
population, and social states are ranked by a criterion of
Sustenance-Capacity Dominance that *replaces* Pareto efficiency rather
than refining it. The theory paper is explicit that this is a change of
primitive — “the primitive is society’s sustenance capacity — not
subjective utility” — and that the resulting criterion is
incommensurable with the Paretian one: Sustenance-Capacity Dominance “is
structurally linear (risk-neutral) in expected” sustenance capacity and
“can rank an allocation as dominant that a Paretian welfare function
would reject, and vice versa.” The core is, in short, *declared* — held
out as a chosen primitive, not presented as a neutral default.

This paper does not defend that choice, and the refusal is principled,
not evasive. Within the Lakatosian framework a hard core is held immune
by the programme’s methodological decision; it is not the object of
test. To demand that the programme empirically *prove*
sustenance-capacity-as-numéraire before its development can be appraised
is to misunderstand the unit of appraisal — it is to charge to the core
a burden the framework assigns to the belt. We therefore claim only two
things about the core, both of which are checkable and neither of which
is a truth-claim: that it is *declared*, and that it was *kept fixed
across the development*. The second claim is load-bearing and is
established in Section 6.2, where it is shown that the core was kept
fixed not by default but under direct and recorded attack.

A clarification forestalls the obvious objection that immunity is a
dogmatic cheat. Immunity is a *revisable* decision (Section 2): a
programme whose core must be rescued ever more elaborately is free to
abandon it, and degenerating programmes do. The asymmetry we will draw
with orthodoxy in Section 8 is therefore not that this programme has a
normative core while orthodoxy does not — every welfare economics fixes
a numéraire, which is the foundational normative act — but that this
programme *declares* its core as a choice, whereas the
competitive-efficiency benchmark typically operates as an undeclared
default. We rest no part of the appraisal on that asymmetry; we note it.

## 4. The protective belt — four plies

The four papers are best read not as four programmes, nor as four
independent applications, but as one protective belt of four plies
around the Section 3 core. The claim that they form a single belt rather
than parallel exercises rests on two observable features, and
intellectual honesty requires stating the second one precisely rather
than asserting a tidier unity than the texts support.

First, three of the four share the core ontology: the theory,
structural, and empirical plies each take the sustenance of the working
population, not utility, as the primitive against which its object is
evaluated. The policy ply is the deliberate exception — it evaluates the
rent brake not on the sustenance primitive but on the orthodox welfare
economics of the second best (the borrowed engine of Section 5), and
that exception is a strength, not a crack, for the reason given below.
Second, three of them are wired into one explicit citation-dependency
structure, with a documented asymmetry for the fourth. The theory paper
(2026c) is the hub: the structural paper sources its sustenance
function, its stochastic-discount factor, and its maintained assumptions
to it, and the empirical paper rests its structural interpretation on
it. The structural paper (2026a) and the empirical paper (2026b) are
reciprocally wired: the structural paper defers its empirics to the
empirical paper, and the empirical paper defers its theoretical
derivation back to the structural paper. The policy paper is the
exception that must be conceded: it cites *none* of the other three, and
is cited *inward* by the structural paper (for its supply-inelasticity
diagnosis and its treatment of the policy instrument) without citing
back. The policy ply therefore belongs to the belt not by the sustenance
primitive but by *shared diagnosis* of the same multi-distorted market,
*inward citation* (the structural paper sources its supply-inelasticity
diagnosis and policy instrument to it), and a *shared policy conclusion*
— a looser coupling than the theory–structural–empirical triangle, and
we do not pretend otherwise. That looseness is the purest expression of
the positive heuristic of Section 5: the policy ply overturns the
orthodox veto on rent regulation using *only* the orthodox apparatus,
requiring none of the programme’s own ontology to do it. The programme
therefore presses on two independent fronts — a sustenance-based welfare
case (the triangle) and an orthodox-internal second-best case (the
policy ply) — so that a reader who refuses the hard core outright still
faces the veto-removal on ground entirely his own. The unit that
develops over time is nonetheless one programme: the hub-and-triangle
structure and the shared primitive bind the three core plies, while the
fourth is bound by shared diagnosis, inward citation, and the same
immanent-critique heuristic carried to its limit.

- **Ply 1 — the value theory.** A structural macro-finance model in
  which the sustenance of the working population is the primitive, the
  stochastic discount factor is derived from the Bellman multipliers on
  sustenance capacity rather than posited, and a closed-form structural
  risk premium is obtained (Theorem 4.1, discussed in Section 6). This
  ply supplies the formal core and the welfare criterion.
- **Ply 2 — the structural derivation.** From the dominance criterion, a
  sustenance-consistent rent ceiling is derived, *conditional on a
  maintained empirical premise* that the housing asset is overvalued
  relative to its sustenance-consistent price. The conditionality is
  stated in the paper, not concealed.
- **Ply 3 — the empirical identification.** A difference-in-differences
  study of the capitalisation of a housing subsidy into rents, reporting
  robust directional results across many specifications.
- **Ply 4 — the policy defence.** An argument that, in a market whose
  competitive preconditions are demonstrably absent, the orthodox
  second-best objection to rent regulation is inapplicable — and,
  symmetrically, that deregulation enjoys no first-best presumption
  either.

The plies exhibit what may be called four honesties: each declares a
specific *non-result* at the exact point where overclaiming would be
most tempting. The structural ply declares its overvaluation premise a
maintained premise. The empirical ply reports its headline coefficient
as a lower bound and concedes an observational equivalence its data
cannot break. The policy ply holds its central theorem to its strictly
negative form, claiming non-exclusion rather than optimality. The theory
ply distinguishes what it proves (a conditional risk-premium theorem)
from what it defers (the criterion’s full axiomatics). These honesties
are not modesty for its own sake; Section 6 shows they are the evidence
of *non-degeneration*, and must be kept rigorously distinct from the
evidence of *content-increase*, with which a careless reading would
conflate them.

## 5. The positive heuristic — immanent critique with borrowed engines

The belt is not developed opportunistically. It is developed by a single
rule: take an instrument the orthodox tradition trusts, and turn it on
the tradition’s own conclusion. This is immanent critique in the strict
sense — not an external denunciation of the mainstream from foreign
premises, but an internal demonstration that the mainstream’s own
apparatus, applied to its own object, yields a conclusion the mainstream
disfavours. The theory paper states the strategy without euphemism: it
pursues “deliberate methodological immanence,” in which “the same
Bellman apparatus that standard macro deploys to establish the
efficiency of competitive institutions is here turned to demonstrate
that those institutions generate structurally necessary violations of
the structural welfare criterion.” Competitive institutions, in the
model, “do not fail … by failing to solve their own Paretian
optimization problem but by solving it perfectly.”

The heuristic is instantiated four times with four borrowed engines:[^2]
the asset-pricing / perturbation apparatus in the theory ply; the
planner’s constrained-optimisation problem in the structural ply; the
difference-in-differences design — the workhorse of orthodox programme
evaluation — in the empirical ply; and the general theory of the second
best together with the Diamond–Mirrlees production-efficiency result in
the policy ply. That the heuristic is *rule-governed* in this way is
itself an appraisal-relevant fact: a programme whose belt grows by a
stated heuristic is one whose growth can be assessed for excess content,
rather than dismissed as post-hoc fitting.

A standing objection must be registered: does adopting the rival’s
apparatus concede the rival’s frame? The programme’s answer is that the
indictment targets the institutions the apparatus *certifies*, not the
apparatus’s internal logic — and that inhabiting the frame is precisely
what makes the verdict binding rather than question-begging. We report
this as the programme’s heuristic and as the source of its critical
reach; we do not here advance it as a universal method, an ambition
Section 8 records as deferred.

## 6. The appraisal — theoretically progressive, non-degenerating, empirically progressing

We now apply Lakatos’s graded criterion. The verdict is deliberately
split into three, because the artifacts support the three components to
different degrees, and collapsing them into a single “the programme is
progressive” would be the precise auto-goal this paper exists to avoid.

### 6.1 Theoretical progressivity: a content-increasing development step

The decisive question under Lakatos is not whether the programme
*possesses* a sharp result — a sharp result shows only that its content
is high, not that its development was progressive — but whether a
*revision of the belt added content under pressure*. The programme
furnishes one clean, documented instance, and it is best presented as a
development step rather than as a founding endowment.

In an earlier state, the theory ply contained only a *conjecture* about
the demographic component of the risk premium: the second covariance
term was identified but its coefficient was unproven and its derivation
explicitly deferred. Under subsequent review the conjecture was
discharged into a conditional theorem (Theorem 4.1) with a closed-form
coefficient and a signed result. The structural risk premium takes the
form

$$
\mathbb{E}_t[R_{K,t+1}] - R_{f,t} \approx \sigma\gamma\,\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta\tilde{c}_{t+1}, R_{K,t+1})
+ (1-\gamma)\,\Omega\,\operatorname{Cov}_t(\Delta n_{t+2}, R_{K,t+1}),
$$

with the demographic-risk coefficient pinned in closed form,

$$
(1-\gamma)\,\Omega \;=\; -\,\frac{\psi_x\,\xi_\theta}{\gamma\,\varphi_\theta},
$$

where $\psi_x$ is a Bellman curvature elasticity, $\xi_\theta$ the net
capital-ratio savings response, $\varphi_\theta$ the consumption
response, and $\gamma$ the sustenance elasticity.

What makes this a *content-increasing* step, and not an ad hoc rescue,
is that the revision *removed degrees of freedom*. Standard
consumption-CAPM can match any observed risk premium by freely
calibrating the risk-aversion parameter. The revised belt binds the
effective risk price to physical sustenance-technology parameters: the
modeller is no longer free to choose it.[^3] A revision that converts a
free parameter into a constrained, in-principle-testable function of
named primitives is the structural opposite of a degenerating manoeuvre,
which would instead add free parameters to absorb an anomaly. The
exhibit, properly stated, is the *transition* from conjecture to signed
theorem; the static theorem alone would not establish progressivity,
only high content.

Two clarifications pre-empt predictable objections. First, this is an
appraisal of a *logical* relation between two public states of an
artifact — an earlier draft in which the coefficient was an unproven
conjecture, and a later one in which it is a signed closed-form
restriction — not a claim about the authors’ psychology or the
wall-clock interval between them. In Popper’s terms the relation lives
in World 3 (the objective content of the theories), not World 2 (the
private process of authoring); that the transition happened quickly, or
within a short span of one programme’s development, neither establishes
nor undermines it. The objection “this is mere intra-draft editing” is
therefore a category error: editing is a World-2 activity,
content-increase is a World-3 property, and the second is read off the
two artifact-states regardless of how the first proceeded. Second, the
restriction is not merely *tidier*; it *forbids more*. The conjecture
permitted any value of the demographic risk loading; the theorem ties
it, by a cross-equation identity, to independently estimable
elasticities ($\psi_x,\xi_\theta,\varphi_\theta,\gamma$), so that ranges
of jointly-estimated values which the conjecture allowed are now ruled
out. Excess testable content, in the precise Popperian sense of saying
more by forbidding more, is exactly what this delivers — whether or not
the forbidding has yet been put to data (Section 6.3).

One scope limit must be stated plainly, on pain of the auto-goal this
paper exists to avoid. A single content-increasing step (with one
reinforcing internal exhibit) shows that the programme *has begun* a
progressive problemshift; it does not show a *track record* of repeated
such steps, which is what a mature programme accumulates. This is why
the paper claims, in its title and throughout, *a* progressing
problemshift — an indefinite article doing deliberate work — and not an
established progressive programme. The verdict is “progression underway
and non-degenerating,” not “progressivity proven over a development
series.”

A second, purely internal exhibit reinforces the point: in the
structural ply, a value-flow separability assumption converts an
ambiguous interior optimum into a corner solution that fixes the policy
parameter — replacing an indeterminate object with a falsifiable one
under explicitly stated maintained assumptions. Again, determinacy
increases and modelling freedom decreases.

It must be stated precisely what this does and does not show. The
restriction’s excess content over the *orthodox rival* (consumption-CAPM
cannot generate such a cross-equation tie) discharges the heuristic
obligation of Section 5. Its status as a conjecture-to-theorem
*transition* discharges the progressivity obligation of this section.
These are two distinct claims about the same artifact and are kept
separate; neither is a claim that the restriction is empirically
*corroborated*, which is the subject of Section 6.3.

### 6.2 Non-degeneration: the core was attacked, not merely kept

A programme is degenerating if it saves its core by proliferating
untestable auxiliaries. This programme did the opposite on two counts.

First, under attack the belt *restricted its own claims* rather than
inflating them. The empirical coefficient was declared a lower bound;
the structural overvaluation claim was narrowed to a maintained premise
with explicit scope; an observational equivalence was conceded; the
policy theorem was held to its negative form. These are *retreats*, not
content-increases, and it would be an auto-goal to count them as
progress. Their correct Lakatosian role is different and specific: they
show that when the programme met resistance, it responded by honestly
*restricting scope*, not by manufacturing auxiliary hypotheses to rescue
an overclaim. That is the signature of non-degeneration.

Second — and this is what converts “the core was kept fixed” from a
triviality into evidence — the core was *genuinely attacked* and
survived without mutation. The construction record and cross-draft
comparison document recurrent, direct assaults on the core itself:
objections pressing the programme to collapse its dominance criterion
back into a Paretian or utilitarian one (recorded variously as the
“dual-welfare schism” objection, the “unify-the-planner” demand, and the
“schizophrenic planner” objection — each, in effect, an argument that
the programme cannot maintain a non-Paretian welfare primitive).[^4]
Each was met by belt revision or by principled refusal — for instance,
by showing that the demanded price–quantity duality holds only for a
utilitarian objective, which the programme has declined to adopt — and
in no case by altering the core. A core that survives sustained attempts
to overturn it is *preserved under pressure*; a core never challenged
would be merely *retained*. The evidence supports the former.

### 6.3 Empirical status: corroborated shared content, and the discriminating frontier

This is the sharpest point in the appraisal, and the one most exposed to
an auto-goal. The empirical ply corroborates the programme’s
*directional* prediction (the sign of the fiscal-capture effect) and
sharpened its identification under criticism, through a COVID-window
restriction and a density-controlled specification. Two demotions are
mandatory.

First, sharper identification of an *existing* claim is better
*testing*, not excess *content*; it strengthens confidence in a
prediction the programme already made, rather than corroborating a new
one. Second, and decisively: the corroborated directional fact is, in
the empirical paper’s own words, “observationally equivalent at the
level of the rent-growth coefficient”[^5] between the programme’s
account and the orthodox fiscal-incidence account. A fact that both the
programme and its rival predict is *shared content*, not the
rival-beating *novel* content Lakatos’s empirical progressivity
requires. Corroborating it confirms the programme is not refuted on
shared ground; it buys the programme nothing *over* orthodoxy. To
present this corroboration as empirical progressivity would be exactly
the move the no-auto-goal discipline forbids, and the empirical paper
itself is the source that forbids it.

Where, then, is the programme’s discriminating content? In precisely
what is *not yet* corroborated: the cross-equation restriction of
Section 6.1, which makes a prediction consumption-CAPM cannot, and the
sustenance function’s structural over-identifying restriction on the
demographic channel (a unit long-run elasticity; a single parameter
governing both that elasticity and the stock’s persistence) — the
genuine separator the empirical paper specifies, the first-moment
demographic prediction itself being shared with the rival through income
effects — which it explicitly places outside its own scope. This is the
programme’s live frontier. Its status is a conjecture awaiting test, and
we label it as such.

Hence the bounded verdict: the programme is theoretically progressive
(6.1) and non-degenerating (6.2); its empirical development to date
corroborates shared content and sharpens identification, but its
rival-beating empirical progressivity is *pending*, contingent on a
declared, falsifiable test. The programme is *progressing* — its excess
content is theoretical and its discriminating empirical test is
specified but unrun — which is a defensible Lakatosian status, and a
different and weaker claim than “empirically progressive.” A
theoretically progressive programme with discriminating corroboration
outstanding is permitted by Lakatos and is not degenerating; it is a
candidate whose empirical verdict is open.

Two clarifications keep this verdict honest. First, on Lakatos’s own
account empirical corroboration need not be instantaneous for
theoretical progressivity to be credited: a programme remains
theoretically progressive so long as its heuristic keeps generating
novel, testable, degrees-of-freedom-reducing content, even while the
apparatus to test that content is still being built (Lakatos 1970). We
report the frontier not as a failure of progress but as the precise
condition of a theoretically progressive programme whose discriminating
predictions are now well-posed for the next empirical step. Second, the
observational equivalence does not make the two accounts theoretically
equivalent, and Occam’s razor does not favour the rival: the orthodox
fiscal-incidence model is not *simpler*, it is *narrower* — it predicts
the rent-growth coefficient, and through income effects the first-moment
demographic path too, but is structurally silent on the
*over-identifying restrictions* the sustenance primitive imposes: the
cross-equation relation that pins the effective risk price to physical
parameters, and the sustenance function’s unit-elasticity /
single-parameter restriction on the demographic channel. Silence on
those structural dimensions — not on the first-moment demographic sign,
which the rival shares — is not equivalence. The programme generates
additional excess theoretical content the rival cannot produce. This is
a wider *untested* falsifiable frontier, not a superiority verdict:
because that excess content is as yet uncorroborated, it does not (and
on the no-auto-goal discipline must not) establish that the programme
beats its rival — only that it is not redundant with it.

A symmetric possibility must be granted, because it follows from the
same logic and a careful reader will raise it: orthodoxy might simply
*annex* the cross-equation restriction — treat the demographic risk
loading as an interesting friction bolted onto an otherwise unchanged
consumption-CAPM — while discarding the structural core that motivated
it. This would be no embarrassment to the present claim, and it is worth
seeing why. If the restriction is adopted and corroborated, that is the
excess content vindicated as content the rival previously lacked, which
is Lakatosian progress *for the content*. What annexation would threaten
is the programme’s *distinctness* — its standing as a separate programme
rather than a tributary feeding the mainstream — not its
*progressivity*. Those are different appraisals, and this paper makes
only the second. That the programme’s most testable contribution is also
the one most liable to absorption by its rival is a fact about its
porous boundary, recorded here, not a defect in the progressivity
verdict.

## 7. The construction record as bounded corroboration

This reconstruction has access to something most rational
reconstructions lack: a contemporaneous record of the programme’s
development, in which the belt revisions of Section 6 and the
core-attacks of Section 6.2 were logged as they occurred. The record’s
evidential role must be bounded carefully, because a development log is
a record of *authoring*, not of scientific discovery, and Lakatos’s own
historiography has been criticised precisely for selective narrative
reconstruction.

We therefore assign the record a strictly secondary role: the published
papers are the primary evidence, and the record corroborates only the
*temporal order* of revisions that the papers independently display. The
claim is not that the record “lets the appraisal rest on observed
history” — that would invite the selective-narrative charge — but that,
where the papers show a conjecture in one state and a theorem in a later
state, the record corroborates that the second followed the first under
documented adversarial pressure rather than being present from the
outset. The contribution of the record is *checkability* by a third
party, not historiographic authority.

The same record makes the programme’s single open seam impossible to
overlook, which is itself a mark in the programme’s favour: the deferred
discriminating test of Section 6.3 — the sustenance function’s
structural over-identifying restriction — is named in the programme’s
own papers as the test it has not run. The frontier is declared, not
hidden, and declaring it is part of the appraisal rather than a
concession against it.

## 8. Scope, limits, and the conflict of interest

The reconstruction’s reach is bounded, and stating the bounds is
required by the very discipline it appraises.

First, and most importantly, the appraisal is not a truth-claim about
the core. That the programme is theoretically progressing says nothing
about whether sustenance capacity is the right numéraire or whether
Sustenance-Capacity Dominance is the right welfare criterion. The
framework holds the core immune; the appraisal lives entirely in the
belt.

Second, the most dangerous objection is one this paper cannot refute,
only scope honestly. Lakatos’s fullest sense of progress requires a
*corroborated novel fact*; by the programme’s own admission its
discriminating predictions are as yet uncorroborated. A determined
critic may therefore say that the programme is, at most, theoretically
progressive and empirically *unappraised* — a candidate for a
progressive problemshift rather than a confirmed one. We accept this. It
is the reason the paper’s title claims a *progressing* problemshift
rather than a *progressive* one, and the reason the deferred demographic
test is presented as the declared commitment whose outcome would settle
the question. The objection is converted from a refutation into the
programme’s stated research frontier; it is not dissolved.

Third, the conflict of interest is real: the author is reconstructing a
programme he authored. Three mitigations are offered, and their
sufficiency is left for the reader to judge. (i) The appraisal rests
only on checkable artifacts — the published papers, and secondarily the
construction record — so a hostile reader can re-run the reconstruction
on the same artifacts and reach a different verdict; the appraisal is
exposed to exactly that test, and the author actively *invites* an
independent reconstruction as the proper remedy for residual authorial
selection. (ii) The paper reports the programme’s open seam (Sections
6.3, 7) rather than only its successes, and demotes the programme’s most
convenient empirical result (Section 6.3). (iii) The appraisal criterion
is general and applies symmetrically: an orthodox programme that
declared its competitive-efficiency core and exposed its belt to the
same scrutiny would pass, and a heterodox programme that concealed its
core or rescued it ad hoc would fail. We note, without resting the paper
on it, that the orthodox competitive benchmark typically operates as an
*undeclared* default — the asymmetry of Section 3; the point is not that
orthodoxy lacks a normative numéraire but that its revealed-preference
axiomatics derive a utility *representation* while leaving the *choice*
of Pareto efficiency as the welfare standard as undeclared as ever. A
full development of that comparative claim is deferred.

Fourth, the appraisal is explicitly *framework-relative*. The verdict
“theoretically progressive and non-degenerating” is rendered by
Lakatos’s criterion; a different appraisal apparatus could return a
different verdict, and we do not hide this. A Laudan problem-solving
appraisal, in particular, might be harsher — it would weigh the
conceptual problems the programme creates (how to measure sustenance
capacity; how to operationalise the dominance criterion) against its
problem-solving rate, and a thin early belt could score poorly on that
ledger. We choose Lakatos for the scoping reasons of Section 2, and
concede that the choice is not verdict-neutral; a companion appraisal
under a rival framework is legitimate further work, not a threat the
present paper must pre-emptively defeat.

Fifth, and most uncomfortably, the paper’s own transparency can be
turned against it. A reviewer disposed to refuse heterodox work
appraisal *a priori* — the disposition Section 1 names — can cite the
candid concessions here (the pending empirical test, the single
content-increasing step, the conflict of interest) as grounds for
rejection, and so use the paper’s honesty as the instrument of the very
threshold-refusal it protests. We record this without weaponising it: it
would be self-sealing, and therefore a violation of this paper’s own
discipline, to treat any rejection as confirmation of the thesis. The
honest statement is narrower and falsifiable — that *this* paper’s
disclosures are the kind of material an adversarial reading can use to
raise the cost of an honest programme relative to a concealed one. That
asymmetry of cost is an observation about the sociology of appraisal,
offered as such; nothing in the progressivity verdict depends on it.

That deferred claim — that a *declared* normative core, disciplined by a
falsifiable belt and an immanent heuristic, is thereby held to a
*higher* evidential standard than a concealed one — is a natural
extension of this reconstruction and a more ambitious thesis than the
present one. It is flagged as further work precisely so that it does not
inflate the bounded claim made here.

## 9. Conclusion

A five-paper heterodox programme — a four-ply belt plus this
reconstruction — admits a Lakatosian appraisal in terms of one declared
hard core, a four-ply falsifiable belt, and one immanent heuristic; and,
judged by Lakatos’s own graded criterion, its development to date is
theoretically progressive and non-degenerating, with its rival-beating
empirical progressivity specified but pending. None of this asserts the
truth of the core or the correctness of the policy.

The modesty is the result, not a failure of nerve. It is the largest
claim the public artifacts support without auto-goaling the programme’s
core — and it is enough to do the one thing heterodox work is usually
denied: to place the programme in a form where its merits and demerits
can be appraised on the evidence, rather than refused at the threshold.
Whether that clears the bar for publication is for the reader to judge;
we claim only that it is honest. The case also yields a small piece of
appraisal vocabulary — the distinction between a *progressing*
problemshift (theoretical content-increase under way, discriminating
corroboration declared but pending) and an established *progressive*
one. It also shows that the distinction can be drawn against checkable
artifacts without either inflating the weaker claim into the stronger or
retreating into mere description. A reviewer disposed to refuse
heterodox work appraisal will find in these pages the materials to do
so; a reviewer willing to appraise it will find a programme that has
begun to move, by the mainstream philosophy of science’s own criterion,
and has said exactly how far. That a body of heterodox economics can be
rendered appraisable in those terms at all — against a checkable record,
without overclaiming — is the contribution offered here for the reader’s
judgement.
