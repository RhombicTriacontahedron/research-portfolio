# Two-Input Continuation and the Labour-Accounting Interval
Carlos Galindo Escajeda
2026-09-01

# Abstract

This paper derives what a labour account can measure once the reproduction of the working class takes two inputs, the second a durable public stock funded only by a tax on profits. At an interior rest point, a labour-only charge conserves production exactly when replacement takes less than the product, and under any defensible rule it conserves reproduction only inside a growth window narrower than the capital share. No total adding cash and provision with positive weights on both represents the worker’s continuation index. What his period yields beyond its cost is owner consumption plus the net growth of both stocks, and no rule removes that remainder in a growing economy: its ratio to the charge is an interval, degenerate only when public investment is zero. One fiscal share sets the width of that interval and of three further objects, and moving the tax from cash towards the stock widens it more than in proportion. The same move widens the measured gap between the two classes’ private consumption wherever continuation puts weight on the public input. Forbidding the good’s magnitude to drift between dates is exactly the closure condition.

**Keywords:** net social wage; labour accounting; public provision; reproduction of the working class; fiscal incidence

# 1. Introduction

The oldest way to price the upkeep of a worker is to count labour. Assign to each good the labour performed in making it, add the labour embodied in the tools worn out along the way, continue down the chain of inputs, and the series converges: the good carries a labour magnitude of its own. Do the same for the basket a worker consumes and the result is the labour cost of his period, the classical value of labour-power, and what he performs beyond it is surplus. Classical and Sraffian accounts can do this because subsistence is a bundle the worker purchases at a price. One set of units then balances both books, what it takes to make goods and what it takes to make the people who make them, so conservation of labour magnitudes in the making of goods and conservation of labour magnitudes in the making of workers are one requirement written twice, and the ratio of surplus to necessary labour is a scalar.

That coextension fails as soon as continuation takes two inputs and the second is a durable public stock financed only out of capital income. Public schooling, public health and public childcare are supplied in kind, and the worker does not buy them. Cash paid from the same tax is not the stock, and the sum of the cash and the provision is not what sustains him. A labour-only charge that balances the making of goods then does not balance the making of workers, except at growth factors confined to a bounded band. What it leaves over has a closed form: owner consumption plus the net enlargement of both stocks.

The question is live because the programme that measures the working class’s fiscal position turns on exactly that seam. Charitou, Maniatis and Passas (2025) report, in their own summary of a nine-country panel, that the net fiscal position of the working class is negative in eight countries when indirect taxes are assumed to be partly paid by labour, and that the finding reverses when that assumption is dropped. Their convention is the incidence of taxes attributed to labour. Moos and Qi (2020) treat that incidence as a range: actual cases lie between two extremes, and they use the labour share to choose a point between them. The convention at issue below is a different one: whether the public stock is charged to a worker’s period as upkeep or as gross investment. The range it produces is derived from the laws of motion rather than imposed, and the point inside it is left unchosen. Those measurements rest on a longer line of work that put the object into the national accounts and reports it as a point: Tonak (1987) for the United States, Shaikh and Tonak (1994) for the method, Maniatis and Passas (2019) across welfare regimes, and Karabacak and Tonak (2022) for Turkey. What separates the account below from all of them is that the object here has a width. The only tax base here is capital income, so the statistic those authors compute is not defined in this model, and nothing below explains a panel or a time series.

Four results follow, and each closes a retreat the one before it leaves open. First, no total that adds the worker’s private consumption and the public stock with positive weights on both ranks his continuation bundles as the two-input index does, whatever weight the technology places on the public input. A published total of wages plus benefits is therefore refused as a representation of the thing it claims to measure. Second, the shortfall in the labour account is an identity rather than a residual. Both of its terms, owner consumption and the net growth of the two stocks, are uses of the period’s product that no defensible rule charges to a worker. Third, closing the account confines the growth factor to a band strictly narrower than the capital share, and the band refuses from both sides: below it the balancing charge falls short of what the worker himself consumes, above it the charge exceeds everything the period contains. Fourth, the ratio of what is left over to what is charged is an interval, degenerate exactly when nothing flows into the public stock. Turning the tax from cash towards the stock widens that interval more than in proportion at every rest point that grows. A fifth result stands outside that chain, because it needs no charging convention at all: wherever continuation puts weight on the public input, the same move widens the measured gap between the two classes’ private consumption at every interior rest point, net of the growth response it induces.

Two of these carry further than the rest, because they need less. The refusal of the additive total, and the strict displacement of the worker’s private claim by public provision along the locus where the two classes grow at the same rate, use the shape of the continuation index and nothing else: no rest point, no policy and no law of motion enters either proof, so both hold in any economy whose continuation runs on two inputs in that form. Two further findings are properties of this configuration and are sharper for it. One share of output, the part of the tax receipt spent on the stock, sets the width of four separate objects: the movement of the band of growth factors at which the accounting can be closed, the latitude of the charge against its own ceiling, the interval between the extreme charging rules, and the interval of admissible rates at which a good’s labour magnitude may move between dates. Two of those four carry no growth response at all, so their comparative statics in the fiscal split hold without condition. And forbidding a good’s labour magnitude to drift between two consecutive dates turns out to be exactly the condition under which the account closes, so off the band the books still close, and they close by giving one physical good two different magnitudes at two dates at which the technique, every per-worker quantity and the good itself are identical.

The question behind that statistic is older than it. *Capital* treats the wage bill as one historical form of appearance of the fund a worker must himself produce and reproduce, and holds the maintenance and reproduction of the working class to be a necessary condition of the reproduction of capital (ch. 23). Both survive here intact. What a worker’s period yields beyond the cost of maintaining him is owner consumption plus the net enlargement of the two stocks, and no rule may charge either of those to him; that is the second of them written as an equation, identity (R) below. A stock funded from a tax on profits is then another form of appearance of the same fund, so calling it a claim on capital states an incidence inside this instrument set. It says nothing about whose labour produced the output the claim is drawn from.

Two features of the model would carry a reader the wrong way if left unsaid. The law of motion that brings a class to a settled ratio here runs from consumption to numbers, and that is not Marx’s route. His runs from accumulation through the reserve army to numbers, and the reserve army is used nowhere below, so a reader who takes population to follow from the standard of living is reading an engine closer to Malthus’s. And the composite that continuation runs on is a continuation technology whose public input is paid for out of capital income and enters the worker’s continuation and not the owner’s. Identifying it with the value of labour-power would be a further derivation rather than a relabelling. It bears on the question Starosta and Fitzsimons (2018) place at the centre of the value of labour-power, the use-value composition of the means of subsistence: here that composition includes an input no worker buys.

Section 2 sets out the two-class laws of motion and the two continuation technologies. Section 3 draws what a stationary class ratio forces on the two classes’ inputs, and shows that no additive total represents the worker’s index. Section 4 imposes a labour-only charge on a rest point and locates the shortfall exactly. Section 5 fixes the class of defensible charging rules, derives the band of growth factors on which the account can be closed, and turns the shortfall into an interval. Section 6 dates the magnitudes and identifies closure with the absence of drift. Section 7 signs what the fiscal split does to the measured gap between the two classes’ private consumption. Section 8 concludes.

# 2. Laws of motion

Time is discrete. Two classes have sizes $N_{W,t}$ and $N_{C,t}$. Capital intensity is $x_t=K_t/N_{W,t}$, public provision per worker is $z_t=G_t/N_{W,t}$, and the class ratio is $\nu_t=N_{C,t}/N_{W,t}$. Output is

$$
Y_t=A K_t^{\alpha}N_{W,t}^{1-\alpha},\qquad w_t:=Y_t/N_{W,t}=A x_t^{\alpha},
$$

with $A>0$ and $\alpha\in(0,1)$. Factors are paid their marginal products.

A tax $\tau\in[0,\bar\tau]\subset[0,1)$ is levied on capital income $\alpha Y_t$. A split $\phi\in[0,1]$ sends the share $\phi$ of the receipt into a public stock $G$ and the share $1-\phi$ to workers as cash. After-tax capital income is reinvested at rate $i$. Depreciation rates satisfy $\delta,\delta_G\in(0,1]$. Consumption and the two stock laws are

$$
C_{W,t}=\bigl[(1-\alpha)+(1-\phi)\tau\alpha\bigr]Y_t,\qquad
C_{C,t}=(1-i)(1-\tau)\alpha Y_t,
$$

$$
K_{t+1}=(1-\delta)K_t+i(1-\tau)\alpha Y_t,\qquad
G_{t+1}=(1-\delta_G)G_t+\phi\tau\alpha Y_t.
$$

Write the four shares of output as

$$
\kappa_W:=(1-\alpha)+(1-\phi)\tau\alpha,\qquad
s_C:=(1-i)(1-\tau)\alpha,\qquad
\iota:=i(1-\tau)\alpha,\qquad
\varsigma:=\phi\tau\alpha,
$$

so that per-head claims are $c_{W,t}=\kappa_W w_t$ and $c_{C,t}=s_C w_t/\nu_t$.

Continuation is a technology that converts this period’s resources into next period’s numbers. It is not a fertility choice and not an altruism parameter:

$$
g_{W,t}=\frac{N_{W,t+1}}{N_{W,t}}=B\bigl(c_{W,t}^{\theta}z_t^{1-\theta}\bigr)^{\gamma},\qquad
g_{C,t}=\frac{N_{C,t+1}}{N_{C,t}}=B\,c_{C,t}^{\gamma},
$$

with $B>0$, $\gamma\in(0,1)$ and $\theta\in(0,1]$. The public argument enters the worker’s map and the owner’s not at all. The outer map $u\mapsto Bu^{\gamma}$ is common to both classes and strictly increasing. Every result below is stated for these two functional forms, Cobb–Douglas output, which fixes the factor shares, and the geometric index $c_W^{\theta}z^{1-\theta}$ inside the power map. Lemmas 1 and 2 and Corollary 1(i) use neither form, Propositions 1, 2 and 5 do not use Cobb–Douglas output, and Proposition 5 uses the index alone, without the power map.

An **interior rest point** of the laws is a tuple $(x,z,\nu,g)$ with $x,z,\nu>0$, $i\in(0,1)$, $\phi\tau\in(0,1)$,

$$
g>\max(1-\delta,\,1-\delta_G),
$$

both stock laws holding with constant shares, and $g_W=g_C=g$. Write

$$
m:=g-1+\delta,\qquad m_G:=g-1+\delta_G,\qquad \zeta:=\delta x/w .
$$

The restriction $i<1$ is needed: at $i=1$ owner consumption vanishes, the owner’s continuation factor is zero, and no interior class ratio exists; that configuration is off the domain rather than on its edge.

# 3. Stationarity

Stationarity of the class ratio is the only equilibrium requirement the accounting below uses, and it settles two things at once: what the two classes’ inputs must have in common, and what no single number can represent.

**Lemma 1 (index equalisation).** *Let each class continue through a scalar index of its own inputs, $g_j=F(x_j)$, with $F$ strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$. At any interior stationary class ratio, $x_W=x_C$, whatever $F$ is and whatever the policy.*

*Proof.* Interior stationarity of $\nu$ is $\nu'=\nu\in(0,\infty)$, which is $g_C=g_W$. A strictly increasing map on $(0,\infty)$ is injective, so $F(x_W)=F(x_C)$ forces $x_W=x_C$. No instrument enters. $\square$

Under the maps of Section 2 this reads

$$
c_W^{\theta}z^{1-\theta}=c_C. \tag{1}
$$

**Lemma 2 (displacement, without a functional form).** *Let $x_C=c_C$ and let the worker’s index be $x_W=h(c_W,z)$ with $h$ nondecreasing in $c_W$ and strictly increasing in $z$. At any interior stationary class ratio, $h(c_W,z)=c_C$. If $(c_1,z_1)$ and $(c_2,z_2)$ both lie on that locus and $z_1<z_2$, then $c_1>c_2$.*

*Proof.* Lemma 1 gives $h(c_W,z)=c_C$. Suppose $z_1<z_2$ and, for contradiction, $c_1\le c_2$. Monotonicity in the first argument gives $h(c_1,z_1)\le h(c_2,z_1)$, and strict monotonicity in the second gives $h(c_2,z_1)<h(c_2,z_2)$. Then $h(c_1,z_1)<h(c_2,z_2)$ while both equal $c_C$. $\square$

No differentiability, no concavity and no continuity is used, and neither $\gamma$ nor $B$ appears. A larger public stock strictly displaces the worker’s private claim, and that displacement is an order property of the stationarity locus rather than a feature of the technology chosen to represent it.

**Proposition 1 (the ratio, and the nesting).** *At an interior rest point, $c_W^{\theta}z^{1-\theta}=c_C$. If $\theta\in(0,1)$,*

$$
\frac{c_C}{c_W}=\Bigl(\frac{z}{c_W}\Bigr)^{1-\theta}=\Bigl(\frac{z}{c_C}\Bigr)^{(1-\theta)/\theta},
\tag{2}
$$

*and along the locus holding $c_C$ fixed, $\partial\ln c_W/\partial\ln z=-(1-\theta)/\theta<0$. Hence $c_C>c_W$ if and only if $z>c_W$, equivalently if and only if $z>c_C$. If $\theta=1$, then $c_W=c_C$ at every policy.*

*Proof.* Lemma 1 gives (1). Divide by $c_W>0$ for the first member of (2); solve (1) for $c_W$ and divide for the second. Logarithmic differentiation of (1) at fixed $c_C$ gives $\theta\,d\ln c_W+(1-\theta)\,d\ln z=0$. The map $t\mapsto t^{1-\theta}$ is strictly increasing and equals one at $t=1$. At $\theta=1$, (1) reads $c_W=c_C$, and no instrument appears in that identity. $\square$

So one comparison orders the two classes’ private claims: owners consume more privately than workers exactly when public provision per worker exceeds the worker’s own private claim. The one-input case is nested at the boundary weight, where the public stock has left the worker’s continuation, the two private claims coincide at every policy, and there is no displacement for an accounting to be about.

**Proposition 2 (an unfunded public stock).** *Let $\theta\in(0,1)$. If $\phi=0$ and $\delta_G=1$, then $G_{t+1}=0$ and $g_{W,t+1}=0$. If $\phi=0$ and $\delta_G<1$, any stationary $z>0$ forces $g_W=1-\delta_G$, which is outside the interior domain $g>1-\delta_G$.*

*Proof.* The inflow to $G$ is $\phi\tau\alpha Y$. At $\phi=0$ the law is $G_{t+1}=(1-\delta_G)G_t$. If $\delta_G=1$ then $z_{t+1}=0$, and for $\theta<1$ the worker’s factor is zero at the next date. If $\delta_G<1$ and $z'=z>0$, then $g_W=1-\delta_G$. $\square$

The stock law has one source term, and cash from the same tax does not stand in for it. At a stationary positive stock, the whole receipt paid out as cash leaves worker continuation exactly where no tax leaves it.

**Proposition 3 (rest points, reparameterised by the scale of continuation).** *Fix admissible $(\alpha,\delta,\delta_G,\tau,\phi,i,\theta,\gamma)$ with $i\in(0,1)$ and $\phi\tau\in(0,1)$. For every target $g>\max(1-\delta,1-\delta_G)$ there exists a unique $B>0$ inducing an interior rest point of the laws with that growth factor.*

*Proof.* Stationarity of the intensive stocks is $gx=(1-\delta)x+\iota Ax^{\alpha}$ and $gz=(1-\delta_G)z+\varsigma Ax^{\alpha}$, whose unique positive roots are

$$
x(g)=\Bigl(\frac{\iota A}{m}\Bigr)^{1/(1-\alpha)},\qquad
z(g)=\frac{\varsigma A\,x(g)^{\alpha}}{m_G},
\tag{3}
$$

both positive and strictly decreasing on the open range, because $\alpha<1$. Hence $w(g)=Ax(g)^{\alpha}$ and $c_W(g)=\kappa_W w(g)$ are strictly decreasing, and by (1) so is $c_C(g)=c_W(g)^{\theta}z(g)^{1-\theta}$, which is strictly positive throughout. The stationary condition $g=Bc_C(g)^{\gamma}$ then fixes the scale directly: $B=g\,c_C(g)^{-\gamma}$, positive and unique at every admissible target, with no limiting argument needed. Equivalently $\ln B=\mathcal{H}(g)$, where

$$
\mathcal{H}(g):=\ln g-\gamma\ln c_C(g),\qquad
\mathcal{H}'(g)=\frac{1}{g}+\frac{\gamma\alpha}{(1-\alpha)m}+\frac{\gamma(1-\theta)}{m_G}>0 .
\tag{H}
$$

The derivative follows from $\ln c_C=\theta\ln\kappa_W+\ln w+(1-\theta)(\ln\varsigma-\ln m_G)$ together with $d\ln w/dg=-\alpha/[(1-\alpha)m]$, which is (3). Every term of $\mathcal{H}'$ is positive at an interior rest point, so $\mathcal{H}$ is strictly increasing — the fact Theorem 1 uses. No claim is made about its limits at the ends of the range, and none is needed: uniqueness of $B$ is the displayed formula. The class ratio $\nu=s_C w(g)/c_C(g)$ is then residual and positive. $\square$

Proposition 3 reparameterises rest points of the laws by the scale of continuation, and it is used below to turn a stated tuple into an induced one. A growth factor is solved by the rest point rather than chosen, so a configuration that satisfies an inequality counts only once some admissible primitive puts a rest point there.

**Proposition 4 (adding-up).** *At any interior rest point, $\kappa_W+s_C+\iota+\varsigma=1$. Stationarity gives $\iota w=\delta x+(g-1)x$ and $\varsigma w=\delta_G z+(g-1)z$, and therefore*

$$
w=c_W+c_C\nu+\delta x+(g-1)x+\delta_G z+(g-1)z .
\tag{4}
$$

*Proof.* Expanding the four shares,

$$
(1-\alpha)+(1-\phi)\tau\alpha+(1-i)(1-\tau)\alpha+i(1-\tau)\alpha+\phi\tau\alpha=(1-\alpha)+\alpha=1 .
$$

The two stock identities are the intensive form of the laws at rest. Substitute $c_W=\kappa_W w$ and $c_C\nu=s_C w$. $\square$

Identity (4) divides the period’s product among the claims on it: the two classes’ consumption, the replacement of both stocks, and their net enlargement. It is what makes the shortfall of the next section exact rather than residual, because every term a charging rule might reach is already named in it.

**Proposition 5 (the index admits no additive total).** *Let $S=p_c c_W+p_z z$ with $p_c,p_z>0$. Then for **every** $\theta\in(0,1]$, $S$ and $c_W^{\theta}z^{1-\theta}$ induce distinct ordinal rankings on an open set of bundles in $\mathbb{R}_{++}^{2}$. Coincidence requires $p_z=0$, which is the degenerate total that has already dropped the public stock.*

*Proof.* For $\theta\in(0,1)$, level sets of the index satisfy $z=k\,c_W^{-\theta/(1-\theta)}$ for $k>0$, and

$$
\frac{d^{2}z}{dc_W^{2}}=\frac{\theta}{(1-\theta)^{2}}\,k\,c_W^{-\theta/(1-\theta)-2}>0 ,
$$

so they are strictly convex, while level sets of $S$ are lines; a line cannot coincide with a strictly convex curve on a nonempty open set. At $\theta=1$ the index is $c_W$, which ties every pair sharing a first coordinate, while $p_z>0$ makes $S$ rank them: at $p_c=p_z=1$ the bundles $(1,1)$ and $(1,2)$ are tied by the index and ranked by $S$. Hence the rankings differ at $\theta=1$ as well. If instead $p_z=0$, then $S$ is a positive multiple of $c_W$ and agrees with the index exactly at $\theta=1$. $\square$

A published total of wages plus benefits is an instance of $S$. It does not rank continuation bundles, and no choice of prices makes it do so while the public stock is still an input. Proposition 5 is an impossibility and carries no positive counterpart: what a long-run configuration does hold, once no additive magnitude represents the index, is a different question and is not pursued here.

# 4. A labour-only charge

Assign an undated magnitude $\chi$ to a unit of the good and take the living labour of one worker over one period as numéraire, $\varpi=1$. Conservation requires each activity to surrender the magnitude of the non-labour inputs it uses up, plus the living labour performed. The assignment is a convention imposed on the rest point. The laws do not derive it, and nothing below pretends they do.

**Proposition 6 (production conserves, and the condition is stronger than interiority).** *Production conserves if and only if $\chi(w-\delta x)=1$. A strictly positive pair $(\varpi,\chi)$ exists if and only if $\zeta<1$, and is then unique up to a common scale; with the numéraire fixed at $\varpi=1$, $\chi$ itself is unique, and*

$$
\chi=\frac{1}{w-\delta x}=\frac{\ell}{1-\zeta}=\ell\sum_{k\ge 0}\zeta^{k},\qquad \ell=1/w .
\tag{5}
$$

*The condition $\zeta<1$ is $g>1-\delta+\delta\iota$, which exceeds interiority by exactly $\delta\iota>0$. At $g\ge 1$ it holds automatically.*

*Proof.* Conservation is $\chi w=\chi\delta x+1$, that is $\chi(w-\delta x)=1$. A strictly positive $\chi$ exists if and only if $w>\delta x$, which is $\zeta<1$; the solution ray is then unique. Dividing by $w$ gives $\chi=\ell/(1-\zeta)$, and $\sum_{k\ge0}\zeta^{k}=1/(1-\zeta)$ converges under the same condition, so the closed form and the reduction to dated labour are one object. Stationarity of $x$ is $mx=\iota w$, so $\zeta=\delta\iota/m$; interiority gives $m>0$, and $i<1$, $\tau<1$, $\alpha<1$ give $\iota<1$. Then $\zeta<1$ clears to $\delta\iota<m$, that is $g>1-\delta+\delta\iota$. At $g\ge1$, $m\ge\delta>\delta\iota$, so $\zeta<\iota<1$. $\square$

The threshold $1-\delta+\delta\iota$ lies strictly below one, by $\delta(1-\iota)>0$. Between it and one lies a band of contracting rest points at which production still conserves; below it lie interior rest points at which no positive magnitude exists at all. What excludes a magnitude at the second is the gross-investment share, not the contraction.

**Proposition 7 (reproduction does not conserve, and the defect is exact).** *Under Proposition 6, reproduction conserves if and only if*

$$
c_W+\delta_G z=(g-1)(w-\delta x).
\tag{6}
$$

*Independently of that demand,*

$$
(w-\delta x)-(c_W+\delta_G z)=c_C\nu+(g-1)(x+z).
\tag{R}
$$

*Condition (6) is one equation in the primitives and not an identity: it fails at $\alpha=3/4$ with $\tau=\phi=i=\delta=\delta_G=1/2$ and $g=3/2$, and it holds at $\alpha=3/4$, $\tau=1/2$, $\phi=1$, $i=1/2$, $\delta=1/2$, $\delta_G=13/22$ and $g=3/2$, where both sides are $29/64$ of output and $\zeta=3/32$. At an interior rest point with $g\ge 1$ the right-hand side of (R) is strictly positive, and at $g=1$ it is $c_C\nu=s_C w$. Wherever the left-hand side of (R) is positive, $\zeta<1$: a technique that reproduces itself is necessary for a positive remainder. It is not sufficient, since below $g=1$ there are interior rest points with $\zeta<1$ and a negative left-hand side. Under a closing rule the remainder $R:=(w-\delta x)-b^{\ast}$ equals $(2-g)(w-\delta x)$, and vanishes only at $g=2$.*

*Proof.* Reproduction takes one worker and the period’s bundle and returns $g$ workers, so conserving it would read $1+\chi(c_W+\delta_G z)=g$; under Proposition 6 that is (6). Its two sides are $17/32$ and $29/64$ of output at the first tuple, and $29/64$ each at the second. From (4), subtract $\delta x+c_W+\delta_G z$ from both sides to obtain (R), which uses only adding-up and stationarity of the two stocks and is an identity rather than a conservation statement. At an interior rest point $i<1$ and $\tau<1$, so $c_C\nu=s_C w>0$; at $g\ge1$ the second term is nonnegative; the sum is therefore strictly positive, and at $g=1$ the second term vanishes.

For necessity, write $m=g-1+\delta$ and $m_G=g-1+\delta_G$, both positive. By (R) and the stock identities, $m\,m_G/w$ times the left-hand side is $s_C\,m\,m_G+(m-\delta)(\iota m_G+\varsigma m)$. Suppose $\zeta\ge1$, that is $m\le\delta\iota$. Then $m<\delta$, the second product is negative, and dropping $\varsigma m$ leaves the expression at most $m_G[s_C m-(\delta-m)\iota]$. Now $s_C m\le s_C\delta\iota$ and $(\delta-m)\iota\ge\delta\iota(1-\iota)$, and $s_C<1-\iota$ because $s_C+\iota=(1-\tau)\alpha<1$; so the expression is negative. For insufficiency, at $\alpha=0.33$, $\tau=0.5$, $\phi=0.6$, $i=0.9$, $\delta=0.08$, $\delta_G=0.5$ and $g=0.932$ the rest point is interior, $\zeta=0.99$, and the left-hand side is $-0.841\,w$; Proposition 3 induces that growth factor, which exceeds $\max(1-\delta,1-\delta_G)$. If a rule sets $b^{\ast}=(g-1)(w-\delta x)$, the left-hand side of (R) becomes $(2-g)(w-\delta x)$, and given $w>\delta x$ this vanishes if and only if $g=2$. $\square$

Closure and a vanishing right-hand side are two events, not one, and they are close enough to be welded together by mistake. Closure is $b=(g-1)(w-\delta x)$; a vanishing right member of (R) is $b=w-\delta x$. They coincide only at $g=2$, which Proposition 8 excludes for every rule in $\mathcal{B}$. A rule can close the accounting and leave a remainder, and in the window it does.

The public stock enters (R) twice, with different coefficients. Replacement $\delta_G z$ stands on the worker’s side; net enlargement $(g-1)z$ stands on the other. Stationarity gives $\delta_G z+(g-1)z=\varsigma w$, and likewise $\delta x+(g-1)x=\iota w$: replacing the stock is part of what the period costs, enlarging it is not. Charge the worker nothing for the public stock and the whole of $\varsigma w$ joins the right-hand side of (R). Charge him the period’s gross public investment and the public stock leaves that side entirely. Charge replacement only, as production already charges $\delta x$, and the stock is split. All three placements are exact, and the laws adjudicate none of them.

The coextension of the two conservation requirements that classical accounting treats as one is therefore an artefact of a one-input bundle. It survives exactly as long as everything a worker’s continuation uses is something he buys.

# 5. The rule class, the window, and the interval

Let $b$ be the physical bundle charged to the reproduction of one worker. The class is

$$
\mathcal{B}:=\bigl\{\,b:\ c_W\le b\le c_W+\varsigma w\,\bigr\}.
$$

The lower vertex charges private consumption alone; the upper charges that plus the period’s gross public investment. The replacement charge $c_W+\delta_G z$ of Proposition 7 lies between them only when the economy is not contracting. Since $\delta_G z=\lambda_1\varsigma w$ with $\lambda_1=\delta_G/m_G$, and $\lambda_1-1=(1-g)/m_G$, it lies in $\mathcal{B}$ exactly when $g\ge1$ and strictly inside it when $g>1$. Below $g=1$ replacement exceeds the period’s gross public investment and the charge leaves the class. At $g=1$ it is the upper vertex, and what it leaves, by Proposition 7, is $c_C\nu=s_C w$: the owners’ consumption and nothing else. The lower bound is not optional: a rule charging less than what the worker consumes is not charging his period. The upper bound is an argument rather than a derivation. The model contains two charges that the period does not itself produce — labour, and the services of the two stocks — and private capital is already charged to production at $\delta x$, so charging any of it again to reproduction would charge one real thing twice. That leaves the public stock, of which the period contains $\varsigma w$ in total. A rule above the upper vertex charges the worker for something the period does not contain. Results that use only the lower bound are marked where they occur.

**Proposition 8 (the window).** *Reproduction conserves if and only if $b^{\ast}=(g-1)(w-\delta x)$. Inside $\mathcal{B}$ this requires*

$$
\kappa_W\le(g-1)(1-\zeta)\le\kappa_W+\varsigma=(1-\alpha)+\tau\alpha .
\tag{7}
$$

*Necessary conditions on any such $g$ are*

$$
2-\alpha\;\le\;g\;<\;2-\alpha(1-\tau)(1-\delta i)\;\le\;2 ,
\tag{8}
$$

*an interval of length $\alpha[\tau+\delta i(1-\tau)]<\alpha$, strictly, at every interior rest point, since $\tau<1$ and $\delta i<1$ there. Writing $u=g-1$ and*

$$
Q(u):=\frac{u\bigl(u+\delta(1-\iota)\bigr)}{u+\delta},
$$

*$Q$ is strictly increasing on $u\ge0$ with $Q(0)=0$ and $Q(u)\to\infty$, so for every admissible tuple with $\phi\tau>0$ the band is a **nonempty** subinterval $[g_-,g_+]$ of $(1,2)$, its edges the unique positive roots of $Q(u)=\kappa_W$ and $Q(u)=\kappa_W+\varsigma$; by Proposition 3 every point of it is the growth factor of an interior rest point, for a unique $B$. No interior rest point with $g\le1$ closes under a rule that charges at least $c_W$. No interior rest point with $g>2$ closes under a rule that charges at most net output; at $g=2$ exactly, closure occurs, and only by charging the entire net product $b=w-\delta x$. Under a closing rule inside $\mathcal{B}$, $R=(2-g)(w-\delta x)>0$.*

*Proof.* Dividing $b^{\ast}=(g-1)(w-\delta x)$ by $w>0$ gives $b^{\ast}/w=(g-1)(1-\zeta)$; membership in $\mathcal{B}$ is (7), and $\kappa_W+\varsigma=(1-\alpha)+(1-\phi)\tau\alpha+\phi\tau\alpha$.

*Lower edge.* Every rule in $\mathcal{B}$ charges at least $\kappa_W w$, and $\kappa_W\ge1-\alpha>0$. A positive pair of magnitudes requires $\zeta<1$ by Proposition 6, so $1-\zeta>0$, and the lower edge of (7) then signs $g-1>0$ before any multiplier is used. Only then does $\zeta\ge0$ give

$$
g-1\ \ge\ (g-1)(1-\zeta)\ \ge\ \kappa_W\ \ge\ 1-\alpha ,
$$

which is $g\ge2-\alpha$. The order matters: the multiplier step is $(g-1)\zeta\ge0$ and fails at $g<1$. Where instead $\zeta\ge1$, Proposition 6 supplies no positive pair for any rule to close, so the two cases partition the interior rest points and the exclusion of $g\le1$ carries no condition on $\zeta$.

*Upper edge.* For $u=g-1>0$, $(g-1)\zeta=\delta\iota u/(u+\delta)<\delta\iota$, so $(g-1)(1-\zeta)>(g-1)-\delta\iota$. If (7) holds then $(g-1)-\delta\iota<1-\alpha(1-\tau)$, hence

$$
g<2-\alpha(1-\tau)+\delta i(1-\tau)\alpha=2-\alpha(1-\tau)(1-\delta i)\le2 .
$$

The length of the necessary interval is $\alpha[\tau+\delta i(1-\tau)]$, and the gap to $\alpha$ is $\alpha(1-\tau)(1-\delta i)$, strictly positive at every interior rest point because $\tau<1$ and $i<1$ there. The interval is therefore strictly shorter than the capital share, and approaches it only as a fully depreciating private stock is wholly reinvested — a limit interiority does not contain.

*Exact edges, and the band is never empty.* Substituting $\zeta=\delta\iota/m$ and $m=u+\delta$ into the middle member of (7) gives $Q(u)$, with $Q(0)=0$ and $Q(u)\to\infty$; and

$$
Q'(u)=\frac{(u+\delta)^{2}-\delta^{2}\iota}{(u+\delta)^{2}}>0
$$

on $u\ge0$ because $\iota<1$. The equation $Q(u)=\kappa$ clears to $u^{2}+u[\delta(1-\iota)-\kappa]-\kappa\delta=0$, whose roots have product $-\kappa\delta<0$; exactly one is positive. Since $\kappa_W\ge1-\alpha>0$ and $\kappa_W<\kappa_W+\varsigma$ whenever $\varsigma>0$, both roots exist and are ordered, so the band is a nonempty interval; it lies in $(1,2)$ by the two edges just derived.

*Remainder.* Proposition 7 gives $R=(2-g)(w-\delta x)$, and (8) forces $g<2$ inside $\mathcal{B}$ while Proposition 6 forces $w-\delta x>0$, so $R>0$ there. Separately, a closing rule of any kind satisfies $R\ge0$, because a period cannot be charged more than it yields; with $w-\delta x>0$ that forces $g\le2$ with no reference to $\mathcal{B}$ at all. The bound is attained: at $g=2$ the closing bundle is $b^{\ast}=(g-1)(w-\delta x)=w-\delta x$, the whole net product, and $R=0$. So $g>2$ is excluded outright and $g=2$ is excluded only by $\mathcal{B}$, which caps the charge at $c_W+\varsigma w<w-\delta x$. $\square$

Three things follow, and they are worth separating. The lower bound $2-\alpha$ uses only the premise that a worker is charged at least what he consumes. The sharpened upper bound uses the class. Drop the class and the general window is $[2-\alpha,\,2]$, closed at the top and exactly as wide as the capital share: a reader who refuses the double-charging argument loses the sharpening and keeps everything else, gaining only the configuration in which a worker’s period is charged the entire net product and nothing at all is left over. It is the plainest statement of what closing the accounting would cost.

The lower bound is the weakest premise, and a reader trained on classical cost accounting will want to charge the wage bundle to production instead, as a cost of output. The alternatives can be written down. Let $q\in[0,1]$ be the share of the worker’s consumption charged to reproduction, $1-q$ the share charged to production, and $p\ge0$ the public charge. The two requirements read $\chi w=\chi\delta x+\chi(1-q)c_W+\varpi$ and $\varpi+\chi(qc_W+p)=\varpi g$. The first gives $\varpi=\chi n_q$ with

$$
n_q:=w-\delta x-(1-q)\,c_W ,
$$

whose positivity is what a positive pair of magnitudes now requires. The second closes at $qc_W+p=(g-1)n_q$, so the remainder is $R_q=(2-g)\,n_q$ and the closing rate is $(2-g)/(g-1)$ at every $q$. Hence $R_q\ge0$ forces $g\le2$ wherever a magnitude exists, and the closing rate carries no $q$ at all: the partition is a convention and the two-sidedness is not. What the convention carries is the lower edge. Closure gives $(g-1)n_q\ge0$, so $g\ge1$; then $n_q\le w$ and $c_W\ge(1-\alpha)w$ give $(g-1)w\ge qc_W\ge q(1-\alpha)w$, that is $g\ge1+q(1-\alpha)$. This is the lower edge of (8) at $q=1$ and $g\ge1$ at $q=0$.

Every $q<1$ should still be refused, and not by appeal to the threshold. Each charges the share $1-q$ of the worker’s subsistence as an input to production and the labour that subsistence sustains as a second input to the same activity. At $q=0$ the result is plain: by (R), $n_0=c_C\nu+(g-1)(x+z)+\delta_G z$, the remainder of Proposition 7 plus the replacement of the public stock, and a magnitude for a unit of labour that equals what is left over after labour has been paid is not measuring labour. The premise is held here for that reason. What rests on it is what this model computes on the class $\mathcal{B}$, from the lower edge $2-\alpha$ to the interval (9) and Corollaries 1(ii) to 3, and not Corollary 1(i)’s identity, the bound $g\le2$ or the closing rate.

The window is non-empty, and it is reached: for every admissible tuple with $\phi\tau>0$ the band is a genuine interval of growth factors at which some rule in $\mathcal{B}$ closes the accounting, and Proposition 3 puts an interior rest point at each of them. What fails is occupancy, not existence. At $\alpha=3/4$ with $\tau=\phi=i=\delta=\delta_G=1/2$, the band (7) is $[7/16,\,5/8]$, and at $g=3/2$ the replacement share is $\zeta=3/32$ and the closing share is $(g-1)(1-\zeta)=29/64$, strictly inside it, so a rule in $\mathcal{B}$ closes the system there. The upper edge binds just as concretely. At $\alpha=\tau=i=\delta=\delta_G=1/2$ with $\phi=1$, the point $g=3$ satisfies the lower edge and violates the upper, the closing bundle being $1.95\,w$ against a maximum defensible charge of $0.75\,w$. Both are exact rational arithmetic in the instruments and the growth factor, which is all the two edges of (7) contain.

Neither configuration is merely stated. A growth factor is not free, so by Proposition 3 each is *induced*: for any $A>0$, $\theta\in(0,1]$ and $\gamma\in(0,1)$ one adds to the instruments above, there is exactly one $B=g\,c_C(g)^{-\gamma}>0$ putting a rest point at that $g$, with every instrument held. The scale $B$ is not recoverable from the instruments alone — it carries $A$, $\theta$ and $\gamma$ — so no numerical value for it is quoted here.

The second configuration is the one that matters for how the result is read. Read with the lower bound alone, the failure of closure looks like a shortfall that faster growth would cure. It would not. Above the window the balancing bundle exceeds everything a rule may charge, exactly as below it the bundle falls short of what the worker himself consumes. The two edges refuse for opposite reasons, and both refuse.

The window constrains a growth factor **per model period**. At $\alpha=1/3$ the lower edge is $g\ge5/3$: sixty-seven to one hundred per cent per period. Read as a year against ordinary capital shares and ordinary growth, the interval is empty. Read as a generation, it is unremarkable. The model does not name its period, so the emptiness at a yearly reading is a remark about an unnamed period length and not an empirical finding about any economy.

**Proposition 9 (the interval).** *Let production conserve, and set*

$$
\varepsilon(b):=\frac{(w-\delta x)-b}{b}=\frac{R}{b}.
$$

*As $b$ runs through $\mathcal{B}$, $\varepsilon$ is strictly decreasing and runs over $[\varepsilon_-,\varepsilon_+]$ with*

$$
\varepsilon_+=\frac{1-\zeta}{\kappa_W}-1,\qquad
\varepsilon_-=\frac{(g-1)x+s_C w}{(\kappa_W+\varsigma)\,w},\qquad
\varepsilon_+-\varepsilon_-=\frac{(1-\zeta)\,\varsigma}{\kappa_W(\kappa_W+\varsigma)} .
\tag{9}
$$

*The width vanishes if and only if $\varsigma=0$, and at fixed $(\alpha,\tau,\zeta)$ it is strictly increasing in $\varsigma$. Moreover $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_+<\alpha/(1-\alpha)$ at every interior rest point with $\zeta>0$. A closing rule would demand $\varepsilon^{\ast}(g)=(2-g)/(g-1)$, strictly decreasing on $(1,2)$ from $\alpha/(1-\alpha)$ at $g=2-\alpha$ to $0$ as $g\uparrow2$; and on $g>1$, condition (7) is exactly $\varepsilon_-\le\varepsilon^{\ast}(g)\le\varepsilon_+$.*

*Proof.* $\partial\varepsilon/\partial b=-(w-\delta x)/b^{2}<0$ whenever $w>\delta x$ and $b>0$, so the endpoints of $\mathcal{B}$ give the endpoints of $\varepsilon$, which are $\kappa_W w$ and $(\kappa_W+\varsigma)w$. The numerator of $\varepsilon_-$ is

$$
(1-\zeta)-(\kappa_W+\varsigma)=(1-\tau)\alpha-\zeta=\frac{(1-\tau)\alpha\,(m-\delta i)}{m}=\frac{(g-1)x+s_C w}{w},
$$

using $\kappa_W+\varsigma+\iota+s_C=1$, $\zeta=\delta\iota/m$, and $\iota w=\delta x+(g-1)x$. Subtracting the two endpoints gives the width. Since $\kappa_W+\varsigma=(1-\alpha)+\tau\alpha$ does not carry $\phi$, write $\bar\kappa$ for it; then the width is $(1-\zeta)\varsigma/[(\bar\kappa-\varsigma)\bar\kappa]$, and $d[\varsigma/(\bar\kappa-\varsigma)]/d\varsigma=\bar\kappa/(\bar\kappa-\varsigma)^{2}>0$. It is zero if and only if $\varsigma=0$, since $1-\zeta>0$ and $\kappa_W>0$.

*Ceiling.* $\kappa_W\ge1-\alpha$ gives $\varepsilon_+\le(\alpha-\zeta)/(1-\alpha)<\alpha/(1-\alpha)$ whenever $\zeta>0$, and $\zeta>0$ whenever $\iota>0$.

*Closing rate.* At $b=b^{\ast}=(g-1)(w-\delta x)$, $\varepsilon^{\ast}=1/(g-1)-1=(2-g)/(g-1)$, with derivative $-1/(g-1)^{2}<0$ and $\varepsilon^{\ast}(2-\alpha)=\alpha/(1-\alpha)$. Multiplying $\varepsilon_+-\varepsilon^{\ast}$ by $(g-1)\kappa_W>0$ recovers the lower edge of (7); multiplying $\varepsilon^{\ast}-\varepsilon_-$ by $(g-1)(\kappa_W+\varsigma)>0$ recovers the upper. On $g>1$ the window and the interval are one restriction written in two coordinates. $\square$

The floor $\varepsilon_-$ is net private accumulation plus owner consumption, measured against the most the class may charge a worker. Those are precisely the two uses of the period’s product that no rule may charge to him, so nothing in the floor cancels: it could vanish only if both vanished, and they vanish together only at $g=1$, which kills accumulation, with $i=1$, which kills owner consumption — a pair that is not in the domain. The floor reaches the split only through the growth factor. Using $x/w=\iota/m$, the floor is $\varepsilon_-=\bigl[(g-1)\iota/m+s_C\bigr]/\bar\kappa$, and none of $\iota$, $s_C$ and $\bar\kappa$ carries $\phi$; so with the growth factor held the split does not move it at all, while along the family of Theorem 1, where the growth factor is reselected, it moves by that one route alone. The route is strictly increasing, $\partial[(g-1)/m]/\partial g=\delta/m^{2}>0$, so the floor’s direction along the family is the direction of $\partial g/\partial\phi$, which carries the sign of $P$ and takes either. The floor rises with the split wherever (14) fails, rests at its equality, and falls where it holds — the one edge whose reversal asks nothing beyond (14), since the width must clear (15) as well, reaching the split by three channels where the floor reaches it by one. The ceiling and the width move directly as well. Two normalisations are in play and they answer different questions: $R/b$ makes the closing condition an interval that either does or does not contain a point, which is what Propositions 8 and 9 need, while $R/w$ makes the floor a share of output, which is what names its composition.

Two remarks fence the interval. The strict monotonicity in $\varsigma$ is a statement about (9) at a fixed replacement share and a fixed ceiling $\bar\kappa$, not yet a comparative static of the rest point, along which $\zeta$ moves with $g$; Corollary 3 does that comparison and returns a stronger conclusion than this one. And the degeneracy is $\varsigma=0$ and nothing else: $\mathcal{B}$ carries no $\theta$, so the width does not collapse at $\theta=1$. What happens at $\theta=1$ is different and simpler. By Proposition 1 the two classes’ private claims are then equal at every policy, the public stock has left the worker’s map, and there is no displacement for the accounting to be about. The interval survives $\theta=1$; the question does not.

**Corollary 1 (the latitude, and where its content lies).** *(i) Let a charge class be any interval $[\,\underline{b},\,\overline{b}\,]$ with $0<\underline{b}\le\overline{b}$, and let $\varepsilon(b)=N/b-1$ against a net product $N>0$. Then*

$$
\varepsilon_+-\varepsilon_-=\bigl(1+\varepsilon_+\bigr)\,\frac{\overline{b}-\underline{b}}{\overline{b}} .
\tag{10}
$$

*This uses no property whatever of the economy that produced $N$, $\underline{b}$ and $\overline{b}$. (ii) In this model $\underline{b}=c_W$, $\overline{b}=c_W+\varsigma w$ and $N=w-\delta x$, so that*

$$
\frac{\overline{b}-\underline{b}}{\overline{b}}=\frac{\varsigma}{\kappa_W+\varsigma}=\frac{\phi\tau\alpha}{(1-\alpha)+\tau\alpha},
\tag{11}
$$

*which carries no growth factor, no replacement share and no scale constant $A$, and is strictly increasing in the split and in the tax.*

*Proof.* (i) $\varepsilon_+=N/\underline{b}-1$ and $\varepsilon_-=N/\overline{b}-1$, so the difference is $N(\overline{b}-\underline{b})/(\underline{b}\,\overline{b})=(N/\underline{b})\cdot(\overline{b}-\underline{b})/\overline{b}$, and $N/\underline{b}=1+\varepsilon_+$. (ii) By (9), $1+\varepsilon_+=(1-\zeta)/\kappa_W$, which is $N/\underline{b}$, so (10) reproduces the width in (9). Proposition 4 gives $\kappa_W+\varsigma=(1-\alpha)+\tau\alpha$, which carries neither $\phi$ nor any dated object; then, writing $\bar\kappa$ for it as in Proposition 9, $\partial[\varsigma/\bar\kappa]/\partial\phi=\tau\alpha/\bar\kappa>0$ at that fixed $\bar\kappa$, and the same computation in $\tau$. $\square$

The division is where the content is, and separating the two parts is the point of stating them. Part (i) is a fact about intervals: any two-input charge class has this shape, and a one-input class, in which the two vertices coincide, has no width at all. It is worth writing down only because a paper that displays $\varepsilon_+$ and the width side by side should divide one by the other. Part (ii) is the economics. The denominator of this model’s ratio is $(1-\alpha)+\tau\alpha$, which does not enter the stationary system, so the growth factor and the replacement share leave the quotient entirely. That matters because the width against its own ceiling is monotone unconditionally, whereas the width itself has to survive the growth factor: raising $\phi$ moves $g$, and the numerator $P$ of Theorem 1’s proof changes sign across the family, so the response of $g$ is unsigned. Corollary 3 bounds that unsigned term: at every rest point that grows it is too small to reverse the direct effect.

**Corollary 2 (the split moves one edge of the closure band and not the other).** *Write $g_-\le g_+$ for the exact edges of (7), the unique positive roots of $Q(u)=\kappa_W$ and $Q(u)=\kappa_W+\varsigma$ at $u=g-1$. Then*

$$
\frac{\partial g_+}{\partial\phi}=0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial g_-}{\partial\phi}<0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\,(g_+-g_-)}{\partial\phi}>0 ,
\tag{12}
$$

*so the band of growth factors at which the accounting can be closed at all widens with the split, entirely by lowering its floor. As $\phi\downarrow0$ the two edges meet — an endpoint Proposition 2 removes from the domain.*

*Proof.* $Q$ carries only $\delta$ and $\iota=i(1-\tau)\alpha$, neither of which contains $\phi$, and $Q$ is strictly increasing on $u\ge0$ by Proposition 8; hence $\kappa\mapsto Q^{-1}(\kappa)$ is strictly increasing. By Proposition 4, $\kappa_W+\varsigma=(1-\alpha)+\tau\alpha$ carries no $\phi$, which gives the first equality. And $\partial\kappa_W/\partial\phi=-\tau\alpha<0$ whenever $\tau\alpha>0$, which gives the second; the third is their difference. As $\phi\downarrow0$, $\kappa_W\uparrow\kappa_W+\varsigma$ and the two roots coincide. $\square$

**Corollary 3 (the split widens the interval itself, and more than in proportion).** *Move $\phi$ at fixed $(\tau,i,B)$ with $g$ reselected by the stationary laws, the family of Theorem 1. The split reaches the width through three channels and no others, and*

$$
\frac{\partial\ln(\varepsilon_+-\varepsilon_-)}{\partial\ln\phi}
=\underbrace{1+\frac{\varsigma}{\kappa_W}}_{\text{at fixed }\zeta}
\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{\phi\,\zeta}{m(1-\zeta)}\cdot\frac{\gamma P}{\mathcal{H}'(g)}}_{\text{growth feedback}} ,
\tag{13}
$$

*with $P$ and $\mathcal{H}'$ as in Theorem 1. The second term carries the sign of $P$ and so takes either sign; it always exceeds $-\varsigma\theta\zeta(1-\alpha)/[\kappa_W(1-\zeta)\alpha]$, and its magnitude is at most $\zeta(1-\alpha)\,\lvert(1-\theta)-\theta\varsigma/\kappa_W\rvert/[(1-\zeta)\alpha]$. Hence*

*(i) wherever $\zeta<\alpha$ the elasticity exceeds one strictly — and $\zeta<\alpha$ at every interior rest point with $g\ge1$, so throughout the closure window (8), whose floor is $2-\alpha$;*

*(ii) the width is strictly increasing in the split under the weaker condition $\zeta/(1-\zeta)\le[\alpha/\theta(1-\alpha)]\,(1+\kappa_W/\varsigma)$;*

*(iii) two conditions are therefore necessary for a reversal, and neither is mild. The growth term must be negative at all, which by $P$ requires the split to be large against the reproduction weight,*

$$
\frac{\varsigma}{\kappa_W}>\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\frac{\varsigma}{\bar\kappa}>1-\theta ;
\tag{14}
$$

*and, since $\kappa_W\ge1-\alpha$ and $\varsigma\le\tau\alpha<\alpha$ give $\kappa_W/\varsigma>(1-\alpha)/\alpha$, the replacement share must satisfy*

$$
\zeta\;>\;\frac{1}{1+\theta(1-\alpha)}\;>\;\max\{\alpha,\tfrac12\} ,
\tag{15}
$$

*the second inequality because $\alpha\theta<1$. Replacement must take more than half the product, and more than the capital share, before the direction can turn at all.*

*Proof.* By Proposition 4, $\bar\kappa=\kappa_W+\varsigma$ carries no $\phi$; neither does $\iota=i(1-\tau)\alpha$. So in $\varepsilon_+-\varepsilon_-=(1-\zeta)\varsigma/(\kappa_W\bar\kappa)$ the split raises $\varsigma$, lowers $\kappa_W=\bar\kappa-\varsigma$ against it, and reaches $\zeta=\delta\iota/m$ only through $g$. Since $\partial\zeta/\partial\phi=-(\zeta/m)\,\partial g/\partial\phi$,

$$
\frac{\partial\ln(\varepsilon_+-\varepsilon_-)}{\partial\phi}
=\frac{1}{\phi}+\frac{\tau\alpha}{\kappa_W}+\frac{\zeta}{m(1-\zeta)}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\phi} ,
\tag{16}
$$

and Theorem 1’s proof gives $\partial g/\partial\phi=\gamma P/\mathcal{H}'$. Multiplying (16) by $\phi$ and using $\phi\tau\alpha=\varsigma$ gives (13).

*The bound.* $P=(1-\theta)/\phi-\theta b$ with $b=\tau\alpha/\kappa_W$, so $P\ge-\theta b$; and $\mathcal{H}'>\gamma\alpha/[(1-\alpha)m]$ by (H), so $\rho=\gamma\alpha/[(1-\alpha)m\mathcal{H}']$ lies in $(0,1)$. The growth term is $\rho\,\phi P\,\zeta(1-\alpha)/[(1-\zeta)\alpha]$, with $\phi P=(1-\theta)-\theta\varsigma/\kappa_W$ because $\phi b=\varsigma/\kappa_W$. Since $\phi P\ge-\theta\varsigma/\kappa_W$ and $\rho<1$, the term exceeds $-\varsigma\theta\zeta(1-\alpha)/[\kappa_W(1-\zeta)\alpha]$ whatever its sign; since $\rho<1$, its magnitude is at most $\zeta(1-\alpha)\lvert\phi P\rvert/[(1-\zeta)\alpha]$. Both are the stated expressions. The lower bound does not carry over to the magnitude: at $\alpha=\tau=0.3$, $\delta=\delta_G=0.1$, $i=\gamma=0.5$, $\theta=0.2$, $A=1$, $\phi=0.05$ and $g=1.797$, inside the band (7), the term is $4.25\times10^{-3}$ against $3.2\times10^{-5}$.

*(i)* At $g\ge1$, $m=g-1+\delta\ge\delta$, so $\zeta=\delta\iota/m\le\iota=i(1-\tau)\alpha<\alpha$, using $i<1$. As $t\mapsto t/(1-t)$ is strictly increasing on $[0,1)$ and $\theta\le1$, $\zeta/(1-\zeta)<\alpha/(1-\alpha)\le\alpha/[\theta(1-\alpha)]$, and the magnitude of the lower bound falls strictly short of $\varsigma/\kappa_W$. The remaining $1$ in (13) is untouched. *(ii)* is the same comparison made against the whole direct channel $1+\varsigma/\kappa_W$ rather than against $\varsigma/\kappa_W$ alone, and *(iii)* is *(ii)* contraposed, $\kappa_W/\varsigma$ being strictly positive. $\square$

The excluded region is real and worth naming. A reversal needs replacement to take more than half the product, and the configurations that produce one take three quarters of it or more. Nothing closes there — closure needs $g\ge2-\alpha$ — and by then there is almost nothing left to charge anybody. The direction turns, in other words, only where the quantity whose division is in dispute has itself gone.

Theorem 1 and Corollary 3 put the same $\partial g/\partial\phi$ to two uses. There the growth feedback takes back the fraction $\gamma(1-\theta)/(m_G\mathcal{H}')$ of the direct provision channel, less than the whole of it since $m_G\mathcal{H}'>\gamma(1-\theta)$, and adds the fraction $\gamma\theta/(m_G\mathcal{H}')$ to the cash channel; its net effect, $-\gamma P/(m_G\mathcal{H}')$, narrows the widening only where (14) fails, and where (14) holds it widens the gap further. Here, at every rest point that grows, it takes back less than the part of the direct effect beyond proportion. The difference is the coefficient the two objects place on it — $1/m_G$ against $\zeta/[m(1-\zeta)]$ — and the second is small for the same reason production conserves at all, that replacement takes less than the product.

The width of the interval is the part of a worker’s period that a convention decides rather than the economy. Corollary 3 says that turning the tax from cash into the stock does not merely enlarge that part; it enlarges it more than in proportion. Provision is a less determinate way of paying a worker than cash is, and the loss of determinacy outruns the provision. For a literature that reports the net social wage as a number and argues about its sign, that is the wrong direction to run: the number is worst defined exactly in the regimes with the most public provision, which are the regimes it most wants to compare.

Four statements now say the same thing about one instrument, and not one of them is that the stock gets larger. The split widens the band of growth factors at which the accounting can be closed at all, and it does so by lowering the floor while the ceiling stands still (12). It widens the latitude inside that accounting in exactly the proportion in which it funds the stock (11), and it widens the interval itself by more than that proportion (13). And by Theorem 1 it widens the measured gap between the two classes’ private consumption. The growth feedback does not enter (11) and (12), and in Theorem 1 it takes back only part of the provision the split buys. What the split does to this accounting, it does by changing what the worker is paid in, not by changing how much there is.

Stated the other way round, one share of output is doing all of it. The same $\varsigma=\phi\tau\alpha$ sets the movement of the closure band (12), the latitude against its own ceiling (11), the width of the interval in (9) and (13), and, by (19) below, the width of the interval of admissible inter-temporal ratios. Two of those four, (11) and (12), carry no growth factor at all, so their response to the split needs no condition on the rest point.

Incidence conventions choose a point in $[\varepsilon_-,\varepsilon_+]$. They do not choose whether anything remains.

# 6. Dated magnitudes

Proposition 6 writes the reproduction requirement with the same $\varpi$ on both sides, although that activity spans a period and the $g$ workers it returns exist one date later. The premise — that a unit of the good carries the same magnitude at every date — is load-bearing, and it has gone unstated. Dating the magnitudes states it, and shows what it costs.

**Proposition 10 (dating, and the drift).** *Let magnitudes carry the date, remain positive, and write $n_t=w_t-\delta x_t>0$. Production within period $t$ gives $\varpi_t=\chi_t n_t$; reproduction from $t$ to $t+1$ gives $\varpi_t+\chi_t b_t=\varpi_{t+1}g_{W,t}$. Together they determine*

$$
\rho_t:=\frac{\chi_{t+1}}{\chi_t}=\frac{n_t+b_t}{n_{t+1}\,g_{W,t}}
\tag{17}
$$

*and leave $\chi_0$ free, so the dated system always solves for positive magnitudes wherever $n_t>0$. At a rest point,*

$$
\rho-1=\frac{b-b^{\ast}}{g\,(w-\delta x)},
\tag{18}
$$

*so $\rho=1$ if and only if $b=b^{\ast}$; in shares of output, $\rho-1=[\,b/w-(g-1)(1-\zeta)\,]/[\,g(1-\zeta)\,]$. The condition does not depend on dating production within the period: dated across it, $\chi_{t+1}w_t=\chi_t\delta x_t+\varpi_t$, the two requirements no longer pin the ratio, and a rest point along which $\chi_{t+1}/\chi_t$ is constant and the magnitudes are positive again has $\rho=1$ if and only if $b=b^{\ast}$.*

*Proof.* Substitute $\varpi_t=\chi_t n_t$ into the reproduction requirement and cancel $\chi_t>0$ to obtain (17). Two unknowns and two requirements at each date fix the ratio and carry neither magnitude. At a rest point $n_{t+1}=n_t=n$ and $g_{W,t}=g$, so $\rho=(n+b)/(ng)$, and subtracting one gives (18); dividing its numerator and denominator by $w$ gives the shares form. Dated across the period, production at a rest point gives $\varpi_t=\chi_t(\rho w-\delta x)$, so $\varpi_{t+1}=\rho\varpi_t$, and reproduction becomes $b=(\rho g-1)(\rho w-\delta x)$, which is $b^{\ast}$ at $\rho=1$. Conversely, at $b=b^{\ast}$ the quadratic $gw\rho^{2}-(g\delta x+w)\rho+\delta x-b^{\ast}=0$ has the roots $1$ and $\zeta+1/g-1$. The second can be positive ($\zeta=9/11$ and $g=11/10$ give $8/11$), but there $\varpi/\chi=\rho w-\delta x=(1/g-1)\,w$, and $b^{\ast}>0$ with $w-\delta x>0$ forces $g>1$, so the labour magnitude would be negative. Positivity leaves $\rho=1$. $\square$

Only the size of the drift depends on how production is dated; its vanishing does not.

The over-determination of Section 4 is therefore the price of forbidding the drift, not a property of the model standing alone. Forbidding it is exactly the closure condition of Proposition 8, and the sign of the drift is the failing edge: below the window $\rho>1$ under every rule in the class, above it $\rho<1$ under every rule. Propositions 7, 8, 9 and 10 are four readings of one scalar.

It is worth saying what $\rho$ is, because it is a ratio of a commodity’s magnitude at one date to its magnitude at the next, and the familiar object of that shape is a discount rate, whether it is imported from asset markets or derived from the problem of sustaining a population.

The route by which $\rho$ arises changes what it can be asked to do. A discount rate, however it is obtained, is a **valuation** object: something is being priced, and an intertemporal trade-off is being solved. $\rho$ is not that. Nobody in this model prices anything, no agent chooses across dates, no preference and no kernel appears, and there is no risk to adjust for. $\rho$ is a **consistency** object — the rate at which a labour magnitude must move for a labour-only charge to balance two activities that share a good. Given the rule, it is fixed by conservation and nothing else.

Two things follow that no valuation object delivers. First, the rate exists whether or not anyone wants it: dating the magnitudes always solves, so the question is not whether an inter-temporal ratio can be had but whether it can be **one**.

Second — and this is the constraint the accounting places on any such rate — the constraint has to be quantified carefully, because two quantifiers are easy to fuse here. Fix the instruments and the depreciation rates. Then *some* rule in $\mathcal{B}$ makes the magnitude date-invariant exactly on the band (7), whose necessary container is strictly shorter than the capital share; and for a rule *already selected*, $\rho=1$ exactly when $b=b^{\ast}$. What does **not** follow is a rule-independent magnitude. Since $\partial\rho/\partial b=1/(g\,n)>0$, the rate moves with the charge even inside the band, so date-invariance is a property of a configuration together with a rule, and not of the commodity on its own. The reproduction process does supply a rate; it does not supply one number, and the rules that make it unity are confined to a band no economy occupies.

The rate is therefore an interval as well, and $\varsigma$ sets its width too. At a rest point $\rho=(n+b)/(g\,n)$ with $n=w-\delta x$, so as $b$ runs through $\mathcal{B}$,

$$
\rho\in\Bigl[\,\frac{1-\zeta+\kappa_W}{g\,(1-\zeta)},\;\frac{1-\zeta+\kappa_W+\varsigma}{g\,(1-\zeta)}\,\Bigr],
\qquad
\text{width}\;=\;\frac{\varsigma}{g\,(1-\zeta)} .
\tag{19}
$$

Charging conventions pick a point in (19) exactly as they pick one in $[\varepsilon_-,\varepsilon_+]$, and for the same reason. One clarification belongs with it, because the phrase invites more than the proof gives: constancy across two otherwise identical rest-point dates is a **necessary** test for a magnitude to belong to the good rather than to the date, and what is shown here is that a consistent magnitude passes that test exactly on the band. Sufficiency would need an invariance criterion across admissible descriptions of the technique, and none is offered.

# 7. The split

Sections 5 and 6 measured what the split does to the accounting. What remains is what it does to a quantity an observer can read off without any charging convention at all: the ratio of the two classes’ private consumption, which the split raises at every interior rest point wherever the worker’s continuation puts weight on the public input.

**Theorem 1 (the split widens the private-consumption gap, growth feedback included).** *Let $\theta\in(0,1)$. Move $\phi$ at fixed $(\tau,i,B)$, with $g$ reselected by the stationary laws rather than held. Then at every interior rest point*

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\ln\frac{c_C}{c_W}>0 .
$$

*The splits at which $c_C>c_W$ therefore form an upper interval, crossed at most once, whose boundary is the split at which public provision per worker overtakes the worker’s own private claim. Simultaneous movement of $\tau$ or $i$ is not claimed. At $\theta=1$ the gap is identically one.*

*Proof.* Proposition 1 gives $c_C/c_W=(z/c_W)^{1-\theta}$, and (3) gives $z=\varsigma w/m_G$ against $c_W=\kappa_W w$, so

$$
\ln\frac{z}{c_W}=\ln(\phi\tau\alpha)-\ln\kappa_W-\ln m_G .
$$

Write $a:=1/\phi$ and $b:=\tau\alpha/\kappa_W$. Since $\partial\kappa_W/\partial\phi=-\tau\alpha$ and $m_G$ moves only through $g$,

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\ln\frac{z}{c_W}=a+b-\frac{1}{m_G}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\phi}.
\tag{20}
$$

The stationary condition is $\ln B=\mathcal{H}(g)$ of (H), which written out is

$$
\ln g=\ln B+\gamma\theta\ln\kappa_W+\gamma\ln w+\gamma(1-\theta)\ln(\phi\tau\alpha)-\gamma(1-\theta)\ln m_G .
$$

Differentiating at fixed $(\tau,i,B)$ and collecting the coefficients of $\partial g/\partial\phi$,

$$
\mathcal{H}'(g)\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial\phi}=\gamma P,\qquad P=(1-\theta)a-\theta b ,
$$

with $\mathcal{H}'$ as in (H) and strictly positive at an interior rest point. Substituting into (20) and clearing $m_G\mathcal{H}'>0$, the numerator is

$$
\mathcal{N}=(a+b)\Bigl(\frac{m_G}{g}+\frac{\gamma\alpha\,m_G}{(1-\alpha)m}\Bigr)+\gamma(1-\theta)(a+b)-\gamma P
=(a+b)\Bigl(\frac{m_G}{g}+\frac{\gamma\alpha\,m_G}{(1-\alpha)m}\Bigr)+\gamma b ,
$$

because $\gamma(1-\theta)(a+b)-\gamma[(1-\theta)a-\theta b]=\gamma(1-\theta)b+\gamma\theta b=\gamma b$. At an interior rest point with $\phi\tau>0$ every factor of $\mathcal{N}$ is positive, so

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\ln\frac{c_C}{c_W}=\frac{1-\theta}{m_G\,\mathcal{H}'(g)}\,\mathcal{N}>0 .
\tag{21}
$$

The map $\phi\mapsto\ln(c_C/c_W)$ is strictly increasing on $(0,1]$, so the set on which $c_C>c_W$ is an upper interval, and by Proposition 1 its boundary is the split, if any, at which $z=c_W$. $\square$

The cancellation is exact, and it is worth stating exactly, because the slogan available here is stronger than the algebra. Raising the split feeds the public stock directly, at rate $a$; the stationary system answers by moving the growth factor, which moves the stock’s own investment margin the other way; and what $\gamma P$ removes from $\gamma(1-\theta)(a+b)$ is exactly $\gamma(1-\theta)a$ — the whole of the direct channel’s contribution **inside the continuation component**. It does not remove $a$ from the numerator, because the two stationary terms carry $(a+b)$ and are positive. So $\mathcal{N}$ is positive for two independent reasons: the worker’s falling cash share, isolated as $\gamma b$ by the cancellation, and the stationary response, which would sign the derivative on its own. The direct provision channel is therefore among those reasons, through the stationary terms; what the cancellation removes is only its contribution inside the continuation component. A dominance argument would have been weaker and a different result.

A wider private gap leaves the worker no worse off: the continuation indices are equalised at every split, and by Lemma 2 the public provision is standing in for the private claim it displaces. What Theorem 1 signs is the measured private ratio, and only along the family that holds $\tau$ and $i$. A reader who measures what a capital-funded social wage does by that ratio will find it widening monotonically, which is the direction such a reader is least likely to guess: what widens the gap is funding the stock more fully, not less.

# 8. Conclusion

The classical labour account works because subsistence is a bundle the worker buys: one set of units balances the making of goods and the making of workers, and the surplus over necessary labour is a scalar. That coextension of the two conservation requirements is an artefact of a one-input bundle. Once continuation uses a durable public stock built from profits, a labour-only ledger that balances goods does not balance people, except at growth factors confined to a bounded band, and the defect is identity (R).

The mechanism is that the public stock enters (R) twice with different coefficients. Replacing it is part of what a worker’s period costs; enlarging it is not. Stationarity fixes the sum of the two at $\varsigma w$ and adjudicates neither term, so the account carries a degree of freedom the economy does not close. Every rule in $\mathcal{B}$ relocates the social wage inside that identity, and none deletes the right-hand side while owners consume and the two stocks grow. That the laws settle the sum and not the division is itself a result, and it is why the object has a width at all.

One share of output, $\varsigma=\phi\tau\alpha$, then sets the width of four distinct objects: the movement of the closure band (12), the latitude against its own ceiling (11), the interval $\varepsilon=R/b$ of (9), and the interval (19) of admissible inter-temporal ratios. Two of the four, (11) and (12), carry no growth factor, so their response to the split holds without condition. Not one of the four is the public stock getting larger; what the split changes is the form in which a worker is paid. The same split also moves a magnitude that no charging rule touches: wherever continuation puts weight on the public input, the measured gap between the two classes’ private consumption widens at every interior rest point, net of the growth response it induces. That ratio registers the form in which the worker is paid and not a loss to him, since stationarity equalises the two classes’ continuation indices at every split.

For the literature that reports the working class’s fiscal position as a number, that changes what the object is rather than what any number should be. The ratio it reports as a point is, in this instrument set, an interval whose width rises with public investment in continuation and collapses if and only if that investment ceases; charging conventions select a point in it and do not decide whether the interval is degenerate. The width also rises faster than the investment that generates it, so a programme that compares points across welfare regimes is least well defined where its comparisons are most interesting. For classical and Sraffian value theory what fails is the reduction and not the arithmetic: the scalar that made the two requirements one was a property of a purchased bundle, and it does not survive an input no worker buys. Marx’s necessary condition on the maintenance and reproduction of the working class survives intact, and identity (R) is that condition written as an equation.

The results hold at a rest point of stated laws inside the constant-share class, with Cobb-Douglas output fixing the factor shares and the geometric index carrying continuation, and every step of Sections 4 and 5 is a statement about a single date. Three of them ask much less. Identity (10) is arithmetic of charge classes and uses nothing about the economy at all. Proposition 5 with Lemmas 1 and 2 use the shape of the continuation index alone, so they hold in any economy whose continuation runs on two inputs in that form, and the published total they refuse is refused there too. Extending the rest would take an existence argument under a wider class of policies, together with a model of the choices these laws stand in for. A theory of value, or a measure of any country’s net social wage, would have to add both.

What a labour account can say about the making of goods, it says. What it can say about the making of workers rests on a division the economy leaves open, and the fiscal form is what pries that division apart. The more of a worker’s continuation the state supplies in kind, the less the question of how much the state pays for has an answer.
